# TRES-0011D — Mesopotamian Household-Resource Historical Pressure Test

**Task type:** independent external historical research / model-boundary audit  
**Context mode:** INDEPENDENT-CONTEXT + project application after research  
**Role:** Mesopotamian social/economic-history research scribe  
**Authority:** advisory evidence only; historical research does not directly create simulation semantics

## Question

Is the current v0 Household resource scaffold merely a crude neutral device for grounding collective action, or does it encode assumptions about ownership, property, household resources, obligation, and economic control that are historically specific enough to distort even a mechanism laboratory?

This task does **not** design the final resource model.

## Independence sequence

### Phase 1 — external history first

Research scholarly literature on Mesopotamian household economic organization, especially where evidence permits:

- household as economic/property/labor organization;
- personal versus household/family/control rights over goods and property;
- inheritance/property transmission;
- household heads / authority over resources;
- dependents and consumption;
- debt/obligation and household responsibility;
- private, temple, palace, and mixed institutional economies;
- diversity by period/status/context;
- dangers of imposing modern individual-property categories.

Prioritize peer-reviewed Assyriology/archaeology/economic-history sources, academic books/chapters, university repositories, and primary-text scholarship.

Use at least 6 substantive scholarly sources.

The Project Seed already cites Jason Ur (2014); use it where relevant but do not let one article dominate the answer.

## Required analysis

### A. Historical specificity of current scaffold

Evaluate these current abstractions separately:

- all grain represented as personal stock;
- no Household treasury/property pool;
- Household access only through explicit personal provision commitment;
- protected personal reserve;
- voluntary office-holder private supplementation;
- commitments terminate with participation/dissolution;
- fixed-rank contributor allocation;
- Household support to participant;
- one head/representative authorizing material acts.

For each classify:

- historically plausible as one possible arrangement;
- neutral abstraction;
- highly period/status dependent;
- likely misleading if treated as general Mesopotamian household semantics;
- clearly contradicted by evidence (only if strongly supportable).

### B. Laboratory contamination test

Ask whether these simplifications prevent v0 from testing the more abstract Seed claims:

- households as persistent higher-order social/economic structures grounded in people/relations/resources/history;
- higher-order action arising through intelligible people/offices/resources;
- individual and institutional capacities remaining distinct;
- household identity not reducing to residence/kinship/property alone.

If the current scaffold can test those claims while remaining historically noncommittal, explain why.

If it cannot, identify the exact distortion.

### C. Dangerous future inference

List conclusions that must **not** be drawn from v0 because of the resource scaffold, such as frequency, ownership norms, household property structure, resource-control prevalence, economic efficiency, class relations, gendered control, etc., if supported by research.

### D. Research trigger for later redesign

Define what later historical research must establish before replacing the scaffold with a richer resource model.

Do not prescribe a final design.

### E. D1–D6 historical coupling

Assess whether any current working decision would falsely universalize the provisional resource assumptions.

Pay special attention to D1 and D4.
