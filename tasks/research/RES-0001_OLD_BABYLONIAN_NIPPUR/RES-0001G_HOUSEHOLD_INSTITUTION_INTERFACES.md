# Research Task Packet — RES-0001G Household–Institution Interfaces

**Task ID:** RES-0001G  
**Commissioned by:** Master Architect  
**Status:** OPEN

## 1. Question

Through what documented relationships did Old Babylonian Nippur individuals and households interact with temples, offices, prebends, authorities, and other institutional structures, and to what extent were institutional roles/resources embedded in family and household continuity?

## 2. Why it matters now

The Seed requires higher-order institutions eventually to remain causally grounded in people, relationships, resources, offices, and histories. Before any institutional model exists, we need evidence for the micro-level interfaces that connected Nippur households to institutions.

## 3. Relevant project context

This is **not** a general history of Nippur temples or government. Stay focused on interfaces observable at the individual/relationship/household level.

## 4. Authoritative project sources

Use the shared RES-0001 context, protocol, and canonical Seed copy.

## 5. Specific research scope

Investigate Nippur-specific evidence for:

- temple offices and prebends: acquisition, service, income, inheritance, sale/exchange, gender, and family continuity;
- persons/households attached to temples or other authorities through office, service, obligation, patronage, land/resource rights, or legal relationships;
- whether institutional roles are transmitted through kinship or household strategies;
- household resources derived from institutional positions and obligations imposed by them;
- boundaries and overlaps between “private” household activity and institutional activity;
- elite/non-elite variation where evidence supports it;
- changes across Old Babylonian political phases;
- documentary bias toward officeholders and property-holders.

## 6. Boundaries / out of scope

Do not design temples, palaces, states, bureaucracies, taxation, or political systems. Do not assume temple organization is simply a “large household”; investigate what the Nippur evidence actually supports and where the household analogy breaks down.

## 7. Required output emphasis

Provide an interface table: person/household → institution/office → relationship type → resource/income → obligation/service → transfer/inheritance mechanism → evidence → confidence. Flag cases that complicate a clean household/institution distinction.

## 8. Authority reminder

Advisory evidence only. No institutional mechanics or canonical abstraction decisions.
