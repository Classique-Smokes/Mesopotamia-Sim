# Research Task Packet — RES-0001H Terminology and Historical Categories

**Task ID:** RES-0001H  
**Commissioned by:** Master Architect  
**Status:** OPEN

## 1. Question

Which Sumerian and Akkadian terms, formulae, and conceptual distinctions in Old Babylonian Nippur evidence are relevant to “house/household,” kinship, affiliation, authority, dependency, property, inheritance, adoption, labor/service, and institutional office, and what semantic cautions should govern our English categories?

## 2. Why it matters now

The project deliberately rejects automatic projection of modern categories. A linguistic/source-critical map will help the Master Architect distinguish convenient English labels from historically attested social concepts.

## 3. Relevant project context

This task is not a glossary-generation exercise. Terms must be studied in attested Nippur contexts and tied to source genre, chronology, and scholarly interpretation.

## 4. Authoritative project sources

Use the shared RES-0001 context, protocol, and canonical Seed copy.

## 5. Specific research scope

Identify and analyze, where securely attested and relevant:

- terms translated as house/household/estate/family and their semantic ranges;
- kinship and descent terms;
- marriage, adoption, heir/succession, and related legal formulae;
- slave/dependent/servant/worker/office terms where relevant;
- ownership/possession/share/allotment and obligation terms that shape interpretation;
- authority/status/office/prebend/service terminology;
- how Sumerian legal/scribal language and Akkadian social usage interact in the Nippur corpus where scholarship discusses this;
- translation disagreements or English words that falsely suggest modern institutions;
- formulaic language in model contracts versus functional documents.

Prefer contextual examples over dictionary glosses alone.

## 6. Boundaries / out of scope

Do not infer a complete social ontology from lexical lists. Do not assume one term corresponds to one stable social entity. Do not make a simulation taxonomy.

## 7. Required output emphasis

Produce a semantic-caution table: term/form → language → literal/common gloss → attested Nippur contexts → semantic range → disputed points → misleading English assumptions to avoid → confidence. Include primary text examples with identifiers where possible.

## 8. Authority reminder

Advisory evidence only. No canonical terminology choices for code/specifications unless later approved.
