# Research Task Packet — RES-0001B Kinship, Marriage, and Household Membership

**Task ID:** RES-0001B  
**Commissioned by:** Master Architect  
**Status:** OPEN

## 1. Question

What does Old Babylonian Nippur evidence show about how households were formed and populated through kinship, marriage, birth, residence, affiliation, and other relationships; how were roles and obligations differentiated by relationship, gender, age/life stage, or status; and what evidence exists for entry into or exit from a household?

## 2. Why it matters now

The project treats relationships as fundamental simulation structure and the household as more than a modern nuclear family. We need historically specific evidence before deciding what “membership” or household formation could mean in a model.

## 3. Relevant project context

Stay centered on individuals and relationships. A legal relationship, co-residence, descent relationship, economic tie, and household membership may overlap but are not to be assumed identical.

## 4. Authoritative project sources

Use the shared RES-0001 context, protocol, and canonical Seed copy.

## 5. Specific research scope

Investigate Nippur-specific evidence for:

- marriage formation, marital obligations, and dissolution where documented;
- parent-child and sibling relations, descent, and kin terminology in socially meaningful contexts;
- women’s and men’s documented legal/economic positions within household-related transactions;
- children/minors, guardianship, widowhood/widowerhood, and life-stage changes where evidenced;
- dowry, marriage payments, gifts, or transfers when they bear on relationship formation or household affiliation (coordinate conceptually with RES-0001D but do not avoid the topic);
- co-residence versus kinship versus property association;
- mechanisms by which people appear to enter, leave, join, split from, or maintain ties across households;
- evidence for non-kin household members where relevant, while leaving labor/dependency status analysis primarily to RES-0001E;
- variation among archives, neighborhoods, and time slices.

Pay close attention to whether legal documents express normative/legal capacity or observable lived practice.

## 6. Boundaries / out of scope

Do not define canonical household membership criteria. Do not universalize Nippur family structures to all Mesopotamia. Do not make adoption/inheritance the main focus; those belong to RES-0001C, though cross-reference them where necessary.

## 7. Required output emphasis

Include a relationship-process matrix with rows such as marriage, birth/descent, widowhood, divorce/separation if evidenced, relocation/co-residence change, and other membership transitions. For each, distinguish direct evidence, inferred social effect, and uncertainty.

## 8. Authority reminder

Advisory evidence only. No simulation rules, mechanics, schemas, or project decisions.
