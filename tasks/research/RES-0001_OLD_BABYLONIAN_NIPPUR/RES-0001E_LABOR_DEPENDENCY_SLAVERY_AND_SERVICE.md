# Research Task Packet — RES-0001E Labor, Dependency, Slavery, and Service

**Task ID:** RES-0001E  
**Commissioned by:** Master Architect  
**Status:** OPEN

## 1. Question

How were labor, service, dependency, and unfreedom organized around individuals and households in Old Babylonian Nippur, and what distinctions can the evidence support among kin labor, dependents, enslaved persons, hired/work relationships, apprentices/service roles, and institution-linked service?

## 2. Why it matters now

The project’s household concept explicitly allows dependents and workers, but modern binary categories such as family/employee/slave may be misleading. This task establishes the historically visible distinctions and ambiguities.

## 3. Relevant project context

Status terminology, legal capacity, residence, ownership, obligation, occupation, and household attachment are separate dimensions unless the evidence demonstrates otherwise.

## 4. Authoritative project sources

Use the shared RES-0001 context, protocol, and canonical Seed copy.

## 5. Specific research scope

Prioritize Nippur-specific evidence for:

- enslaved persons in private/domestic contexts, including sale, family relations, labor, manumission, and status change where documented;
- dependents or attached persons whose status does not map cleanly to slavery/free wage labor;
- household labor by kin and non-kin where visible;
- occupations, service relationships, apprenticeship or training relations if attested and socially relevant;
- gender and life-course differentiation in labor/status;
- mobility between statuses or households;
- institution-linked service insofar as it intersects with household membership or livelihood, while leaving the institution-side analysis mainly to RES-0001G;
- terminology and translation disputes, coordinated conceptually with RES-0001H;
- gaps created by legal/documentary bias.

## 6. Boundaries / out of scope

Do not import Neo-Babylonian temple-dependent categories or third-millennium evidence as if they were Old Babylonian Nippur. Comparative material must be marked. Do not collapse all non-free or dependent persons into one category.

## 7. Required output emphasis

Build a status-dimensions table rather than a single hierarchy: legal status/ownership, kin connection, residence, labor/service obligation, economic dependence, mobility, capacity to own/transact if evidenced, and source basis. Highlight categories scholars dispute.

## 8. Authority reminder

Advisory evidence only. No simulation caste/status system or mechanics.
