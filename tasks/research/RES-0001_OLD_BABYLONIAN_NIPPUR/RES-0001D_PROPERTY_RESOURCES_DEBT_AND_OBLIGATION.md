# Research Task Packet — RES-0001D Property, Resources, Debt, and Obligation

**Task ID:** RES-0001D  
**Commissioned by:** Master Architect  
**Status:** OPEN

## 1. Question

What does Old Babylonian Nippur evidence show about the control, ownership, use, transfer, pooling, division, and encumbrance of resources associated with individuals and households, and how did debt and contractual obligations connect or strain those relationships?

## 2. Why it matters now

The Seed treats households as economic and property-holding structures involving resources and obligations, but does not assume modern individual/private ownership categories. We need evidence for what rights and obligations actually attached to persons, kin groups, houses, offices, and households.

## 3. Relevant project context

Distinguish title/claim, use, residence, income right, inheritance right, and practical control whenever the evidence allows. Do not assume every asset owned by a household member was collectively controlled by a household.

## 4. Authoritative project sources

Use the shared RES-0001 context, protocol, and canonical Seed copy.

## 5. Specific research scope

Investigate Nippur evidence for:

- houses and house plots, fields/gardens, movable wealth, silver/barley, slaves where treated as property, and temple-linked income rights/prebends where relevant;
- sale, exchange, gift, division, lease, pledge, and other transfer forms;
- household versus individual claims and joint ownership where evidenced;
- marriage-linked property and inheritance-linked property without duplicating the full kinship analysis;
- loans, debt, collateral/security, guarantees, repayment/default, and household exposure to another member’s obligations;
- creditor/debtor networks and repeat relationships where prosopography permits;
- property fragmentation/consolidation over time;
- legal transaction evidence versus actual economic behavior;
- wealth/status differentiation visible in the corpus, with strong caution against treating archive survival as representative sampling.

## 6. Boundaries / out of scope

Do not design an economy, currency system, or household budget mechanic. Do not assume modern corporate household ownership. Do not infer market-wide prices or prevalence unless the evidence and sample justify it.

## 7. Required output emphasis

Include a rights-and-obligations matrix that separates: asset/resource, named right-holder(s), user/occupant if known, transfer mechanism, kin/household connection, creditor/institution connection, and uncertainty. Identify cases where modern “ownership” language obscures multiple rights.

## 8. Authority reminder

Advisory evidence only. No mechanics or canonical requirements.
