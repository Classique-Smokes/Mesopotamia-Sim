# Research Task Packet — RES-0001C Adoption, Inheritance, and Household Continuity

**Task ID:** RES-0001C  
**Commissioned by:** Master Architect  
**Status:** OPEN

## 1. Question

How did Old Babylonian Nippur households reproduce or transform themselves across generations through adoption, inheritance, succession, estate division, property transmission, and related obligations, and what does this reveal about household continuity beyond any one individual?

## 2. Why it matters now

The project expects households and later institutions to possess accumulated history and possible continuity beyond particular persons. Historical evidence must establish what continuity actually looked like before any model is designed.

## 3. Relevant project context

Adoption may have social, care, economic, inheritance, or other functions; do not assume a single purpose. Estate division may preserve, fragment, or reorganize household resources. Track persons and property through time where archive evidence makes this possible.

## 4. Authoritative project sources

Use the shared RES-0001 context, protocol, and canonical Seed copy.

## 5. Specific research scope

Investigate:

- forms and documented purposes of adoption in Nippur;
- adopters/adoptees, gender/status patterns, obligations, and revocation where evidenced;
- inheritance rules/practices as attested in actual Nippur cases, not merely general law collections;
- estate division and the treatment of houses, fields, prebends, movable property, slaves/dependents, debts, and other rights where documented;
- succession after death and continuity of obligations/claims;
- care/support arrangements tied to inheritance or adoption;
- household fission, property subdivision, recombination, and continuity visible through prosopography/architecture;
- relationship between legal heirs and practical control/occupation;
- exceptions and disputes.

## 6. Boundaries / out of scope

Do not convert inheritance practices into simulation rules. Do not treat later/earlier Mesopotamian law as direct Nippur evidence. Do not assume adoption is equivalent to modern adoption.

## 7. Required output emphasis

Include at least two deeply sourced microhistorical case reconstructions if the evidence permits, showing people, relationships, property/rights, chronology, and uncertainty. Also provide a continuity-process matrix: trigger → documented action → persons affected → resources/obligations affected → observed longer-term consequence.

## 8. Authority reminder

Advisory evidence only. No canonical design or mechanics.
