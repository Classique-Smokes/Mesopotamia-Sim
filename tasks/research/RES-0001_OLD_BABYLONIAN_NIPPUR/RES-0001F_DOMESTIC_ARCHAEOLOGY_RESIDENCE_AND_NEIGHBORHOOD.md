# Research Task Packet — RES-0001F Domestic Archaeology, Residence, and Neighborhood

**Task ID:** RES-0001F  
**Commissioned by:** Master Architect  
**Status:** OPEN

## 1. Question

What can the excavated residential evidence of Old Babylonian Nippur—especially where texts and architecture can be linked—tell us about residence, household spatial organization, property change, neighborhood relationships, and the physical continuity or transformation of households?

## 2. Why it matters now

Texts document legal and social acts; architecture records material space and change. Their conjunction can test whether textual “household” relationships correspond to residence, property, lineage clustering, or other spatial patterns.

## 3. Relevant project context

Do not equate a building with a household automatically. A house can change occupants, be subdivided, rebuilt, inherited, sold, rented, or host activities that cross household boundaries.

## 4. Authoritative project sources

Use the shared RES-0001 context, protocol, and canonical Seed copy.

## 5. Specific research scope

Investigate:

- TA, TB, Scribal Quarter, and other relevant Old Babylonian residential contexts, with exact chronological/stratigraphic limits;
- house plans, rebuilding, subdivision, access patterns, courtyards/rooms, installations, and artifact distributions where interpretable;
- tablet findspots and archive-house associations;
- documented house sale/inheritance/partition correlated with architectural change;
- residence patterns, possible kin clustering, neighborhood persistence, and mobility;
- funerary/cultic/domestic evidence only where directly relevant and well contextualized;
- the inferential limits of ethnographic analogy and functional room labels;
- discrepancies between textual and archaeological reconstructions.

## 6. Boundaries / out of scope

Do not estimate household size from floor area alone. Do not infer kinship from spatial proximity without evidence. Do not generalize two excavated neighborhoods to the entire city without explicit argument and limits.

## 7. Required output emphasis

Include at least one stratified case study linking architectural phases to named persons/documents if evidence permits. Provide a table: house/context → phase/date → texts/finds → documented people/transactions → architectural change → interpretation → confidence.

## 8. Authority reminder

Advisory evidence only. No spatial simulation mechanics or canonical household-building equivalence.
