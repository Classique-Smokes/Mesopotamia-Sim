# RES-0001 — Source Starting Points

These are **discovery leads, not an exhaustive or authoritative bibliography**. Researchers should verify bibliographic details, seek the underlying primary publications, and expand beyond this list.

## Nippur archaeology, neighborhoods, and text/context integration

- Elizabeth C. Stone, *Nippur Neighborhoods*, Studies in Ancient Oriental Civilization 44 (Chicago: Oriental Institute, 1987). Core for TA/TB neighborhoods, household property, architectural change, text-find context, and prosopography. ISAC publication page: https://isac.uchicago.edu/publications/nippur-neighborhoods
- Donald E. McCown and Richard C. Haines, *Nippur I: Temple of Enlil, Scribal Quarter, and Soundings*, OIP 78 (1967). Excavation publication including private houses of the Scribal Quarter. https://isac.uchicago.edu/publications/nippur-i-temple-enlil-scribal-quarter-and-soundings
- Elizabeth C. Stone, “Texts, Architecture and Ethnographic Analogy: Patterns of Residence in Old Babylonian Nippur,” *Iraq* 43 (1981), 19–33. Verify edition/page details before citation.

## Legal and archival materials

- Elizabeth C. Stone, *Old Babylonian Contracts from Nippur: Selected Texts from the University Museum, University of Pennsylvania* (1976). ISAC page: https://isac.uchicago.edu/publications/old-babylonian-contracts-nippur-selected-texts-university-museum-university
- ePSD2 / ORACC Old Babylonian administrative/legal corpus. Use corpus entries as discovery and identification aids and cite underlying editions where possible: https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/epsd2/admin/oldbab/
- CDLI catalogue and images: https://cdli.earth/

## Adoption and continuity

- Elizabeth C. Stone and David I. Owen, with John R. Mitchell, *Adoption in Old Babylonian Nippur and the Archive of Mannum-mešu-liṣṣur* (1991). Includes a study plus publication/translation of 53 adoption texts and prosopographic material. Verify and use primary text numbers directly where relevant.

## Model contracts / scribal categories

- ORACC, *Old Babylonian Model Contracts*. Important source-critical warning: these are scribal training exercises, not functional transaction records. https://oracc2.museum.upenn.edu/obmc/oldbabylonianmodelcontracts/

## Temple offices / prebends

- Wiebke Meinhold, “Prebends and Prebendaries in Old Babylonian Nippur,” *Journal of Ancient Near Eastern Religions* 19 (2019), 55–70, DOI 10.1163/15692124-12341304. Useful for household/institution interfaces, inheritance and transfer of temple-linked income rights.

## Dependency/slavery — comparative caution

- Laura Culbertson (ed.), *Slaves and Households in the Near East*, OIS 7 (2011), especially Andrea Seri on domestic female slaves in the Old Babylonian period. This is broader than Nippur and must be used comparatively unless Nippur-specific evidence is identified. https://isac.uchicago.edu/research/publications/isacs/ois-7-slaves-and-households-near-east

## Researcher expectation

Do not stop at these leads. Search bibliographies, editions, museum/corpus concordances, and later scholarship. Report inaccessible but clearly relevant sources separately rather than pretending to have consulted them.
