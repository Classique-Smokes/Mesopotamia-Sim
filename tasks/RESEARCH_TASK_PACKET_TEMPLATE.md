## 5. Evidence standards

For historical work, as applicable:

- preserve chronology, geography, archive, and social context;
- prefer primary evidence and high-quality scholarship where available;
- distinguish direct evidence from scholarly interpretation;
- represent meaningful disagreement and uncertainty;
- do not universalize period- or place-specific evidence;
- provide precise citations / provenance for claims;
- state evidentiary limits and confidence.

For technical work, as applicable:

- distinguish established engineering constraints from recommendations or tooling fashion;
- support claims with documentation, benchmarks, experiments, or clearly identified expert sources where appropriate;
- state uncertainty and environment/version assumptions;
- compare plausible approaches against the actual objective and constraints rather than popularity alone.

## 6. Anti-anchoring rule

The researcher is advisory and should discover viable approaches from the problem itself.

Unless this task is explicitly a focused comparison of named alternatives, do **not** infer preference from examples, prior exploratory discussion, or unaccepted ideas. Treat only accepted project constraints and this packet's explicit scope as binding.
