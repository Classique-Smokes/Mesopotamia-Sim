# Working Project Constitution

**Role:** Operational summary of founding principles  
**Authority:** Derived from the Project Seed. If this document conflicts with the Seed, the Seed controls unless a later explicit, versioned constitutional change has been approved by the Human Project Director.

## 1. Project identity

The primary artifact is an autonomous, historically grounded simulation of ancient Mesopotamian society. A conventional game, graphical interface, player role, victory condition, or entertainment loop is optional and downstream; it must not prematurely determine the foundational social model.

## 2. Foundational causal direction

Development begins with **individuals → relationships → households → relationships among households → larger institutions and political formations**.

- Individuals are persistent, causally meaningful actors rather than decorative population counters.
- Relationships are fundamental social structure.
- The household is the first major collective structure and must not be reduced to a modern nuclear family.
- Higher-order structures may persist and act institutionally, but their operation must remain historically intelligible through people, relationships, resources, offices, norms, and institutional history.

## 3. Recursive causality

Bottom-up construction is not one-way causality. Higher-order structures must be able to constrain and reshape the people, relationships, and households that create and reproduce them.

## 4. Emergence over predetermined narrative

Historical change should arise from interacting conditions, decisions, relationships, resources, institutions, environment, and prior events wherever feasible. Scripts may support testing or narrowly defined conditions, but must not replace systems the project intends to simulate.

The goal is not exact replay of known history; it is historically plausible causal machinery capable of producing its own history.

## 5. Historical legibility

Important outcomes should have inspectable causal genealogies to a useful degree. The project should support asking not only **what happened**, but **how the simulated history produced it**. Observability is therefore foundational, not merely a presentation feature.

## 6. Historical research discipline

Historical evidence is contextual and must remain so.

- Evidence from one period, place, archive, or social setting does not silently become universal Mesopotamian behavior.
- Direct evidence, scholarly interpretation, and project inference must remain distinguishable.
- Material disagreement and uncertainty must remain visible.
- Research findings do not automatically become mechanics; translating evidence into simulation behavior is a separate decision.
- Historically grounded mechanisms are preferred over familiar modern assumptions when evidence supports them.
- The household-oriented scholarship named in the Seed is a conceptual lens, not dogma or a ready-made model.

## 7. Layered development and abstraction

Build the smallest meaningful lower-layer system and validate its coherence before extending upward. Higher layers should reuse lower-layer reality where conceptually and computationally appropriate.

Abstraction is permitted, including for scale, but significant abstraction that changes the meaning or behavior of people or institutions requires deliberate review. Scale goals do not silently authorize removal of the individual-driven foundation.

Optimize observed bottlenecks rather than imagined ones. Preserve plausible paths to scale without prematurely sacrificing simulation fidelity.

## 8. Human–AI authority

The Human Project Director owns project purpose, historical ambition, simulation philosophy, priorities, and consequential approvals.

The Master Architect preserves source of truth, interprets exploratory input, identifies decisions, explains tradeoffs, coordinates bounded research/review, maintains coherence, and prepares implementation work. It must not replace project-level judgment with implementation convenience.

Auxiliary researchers and reviewers are advisory. Implementation agents work within accepted specifications and delegated boundaries.

## 9. Conversation is non-authoritative

Conversation is a workspace for thought, including speculation, examples, contradictions, and abandoned ideas. A project decision becomes authoritative only through explicit promotion into canonical project artifacts under the appropriate authority.

Examples and analogies are non-binding by default.

## 10. Unresolved matters are protected

Unresolved is a valid project state. Missing detail is not permission to invent it. Premature certainty is drift.

Temporary assumptions may be used when necessary, but they must be visible, scoped, reviewable, and replaceable.

## 11. Consequential decisions require deliberate promotion

A proposal is not an accepted decision. Silence, lack of objection, repetition, or conversational momentum is not approval.

Authority increases with consequence and decreases with locality and reversibility:

- **Constitutional:** explicit Human Project Director approval required.
- **Architectural:** explicit Human Project Director approval required after accessible explanation and recommendation.
- **Engineering:** Master Architect may decide and record local, reversible choices within accepted architecture.
- **Implementation:** implementation agents may make ordinary local choices inside accepted specifications.

No lower level may silently override a higher one.

## 12. Escalation over silent architectural change

Implementation discoveries that would materially change simulation semantics, persistent identity/representation, subsystem boundaries, dependencies, schemas, abstraction fidelity, global coupling, execution/concurrency model, accepted interfaces, architectural invariants, or significant performance strategy must return to architectural review.

## 13. Modular, versioned source of truth

Canonical knowledge must be modular, explicit, discoverable, and versioned. The project must not depend on one enormous prompt, one chat history, or one agent's memory.

Consequential decisions preserve their rationale, evidence, predecessors, and replacements.

## 14. Mechanical enforcement where practical

Important recurring invariants should progressively become enforceable through appropriate tests, validation, checks, schemas, reproducibility mechanisms, or benchmarks when the relevant architecture exists. Bootstrap does not preselect those mechanisms.
