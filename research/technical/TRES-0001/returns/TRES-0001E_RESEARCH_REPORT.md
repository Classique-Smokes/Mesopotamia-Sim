**Evidence notation:** `[P1–P4]` denotes the supplied context and brief; `[S01–S12]` denotes externally checked primary sources in `SOURCE_REGISTER.md`; `[X1–X8]` denotes the executable synthetic probes in `experiments/`. ## 1. Executive synthesis

The technically defensible answer is not a particular clustering algorithm. It is a separation of **what constitutes household-scale organization now**, **what warrants identity through time**, and **what authorizes collective effects on people and resources**. A single mechanism may implement more than one of these functions, but concealing their distinction would make the laboratory difficult to interpret or falsify. This follows from the supplied requirements, which permit continued identity despite changing constituents while demanding that collective action remain grounded in those constituents and their history. [P1–P4]

An explicit persistent household representation is compatible with emergence. It becomes a primitive container only when its existence, membership, or powers are assumed independently of the social fabric. A derived configuration can instead become a persistent object of reference after formation, retain a historical record, and expose ongoing collective commitments without acquiring unearned control over every associated person. Conversely, refusing all persistent collective representation can make succession and reconsolidation depend on repeated snapshot matching. Neither “always create household agents” nor “never create household agents” is warranted by this research.

The strongest candidates for further comparison are **process-first continuity**, in which recurrent commitments and their transformations support a historical collective, and **relation-first recognition with separate continuity evidence**, in which typed patterns suggest organization but do not themselves settle lineage or authority. A probabilistic recognizer remains credible where ambiguity is consequential and the observation model can be defended. Temporal group tracking, mixed-membership inference, joint-action logic, event history, and multilevel influence–reaction each contribute techniques, but none of the reviewed sources supplies a ready-made household solution. [S01–S09]

Eight small reproducible probes produced **40 passing assertions**, with byte-identical results on a second execution. They establish specific counterexamples, not historical correctness. Property/residence proxies divided an otherwise continuous coordination configuration; an untyped network reunited configurations whose coordination had separated. A Jaccard threshold change from 0.71 to 0.72 changed all 12 turnover decisions. Two different agreement histories produced identical terminal snapshots once origin information was removed. A grounded reservation changed an individual's feasible allocation, while changing an analytical household label did not. [X1–X4]

The remaining probes distinguish numerical stability from justified recognition, preserve differences lost by pairwise projection, expose duplicated evidence, and reproduce a locality problem in standard global modularity. [X5–X8] These results support a narrow recommendation: **make continuity evidence, collective-action backing, and interpretive uncertainty inspectable before selecting the recognizer**. They do not select thresholds, declare a universal household essence, or demonstrate a complete emergent household lifecycle.

The next decisive step should be a small closed-loop comparison of the two leading candidates using the same people, resources, and individual-choice fixtures. Formation, division, and recombination must arise from lower-level changes; the test driver must not directly issue household creation, fission, or merger commands. The current return supplies adversarial controls for that comparison, not that implementation.

## 2. Problem decomposition

### 2.1 Constitution is not a membership list

A household candidate can be described by a configuration of participants and recurring relationships: support, shared use, obligations, delegated tasks, resource coordination, or other historically meaningful ties. The supplied foundation does not identify which combination is sufficient. Accordingly, the recognizer's job cannot honestly be specified as “find connected people,” “find a shared address,” or “find relatives.” [P1, P4]

For research purposes, separate evidence of **coincidence** from evidence of **organization**. Repeated co-presence may be incidental. A recurring arrangement that changes what participants can do, preserves obligations during absence, or coordinates replacement of a contributor supplies different evidence. This is a distinction to investigate, not a claim that explicit agreements or consent are necessary for every household.

Nor is coordination by itself sufficient. A temporary work team can coordinate tightly without being a household. An effective recognizer must be tested against such negative controls, alongside weakly connected but durably supported individuals. Temporal durability, multiple domains of interdependence, and recognized responsibilities are possible discriminants; none is adopted here as a historical definition.

Formation also has two observational dates that need not coincide: when sustaining relationships first developed and when the model has enough evidence to recognize their configuration. Recording only the latter creates apparent instantaneous formation. Backdating a conclusion without keeping the time and basis of that conclusion makes the history look more certain than it was.

### 2.2 Identity is a historical relation, not resemblance

A snapshot answers what exists at an observation time. Continuity asks what persisted or was transmitted between observations. The laboratory must preserve enough history to distinguish them. In X3, explicit renewal of an agreement and termination followed by an otherwise equivalent new agreement yielded identical origin-free terminal snapshots. No method receiving only that projection can distinguish the two histories: identical inputs cannot uniquely reveal the discarded difference. A snapshot containing agreement origins or predecessor references is not subject to that particular information loss, because it already carries history. [X3]

There is no contradiction in recognizing continuity while every initial participant is eventually replaced. There is, however, an evidentiary burden: what sustained or transmitted the configuration during those replacements? It might be continuing support obligations, inherited responsibilities, a recurring coordinated process, or actors' recognition of a successor arrangement. A surviving address or name can be evidence, but cannot be made sufficient merely for implementation convenience.

The reverse danger is unlimited identity drift. Successive high-overlap snapshots can form a chain even when nothing relevant to the original organization remains. X2 deliberately contains no agreement history: it demonstrates why neither adjacent overlap nor founder overlap settles continuity. Threshold tuning cannot supply missing social meaning. [X2]

**Identity and descent should not be conflated.** If one organization gives rise to two distinct contemporaneous organizations, both can derive from it. Under ordinary numerical identity, both cannot be literally the same single entity while remaining distinct from one another. A lineage can therefore record multiple descendants while continuity remains assigned to one, to neither, or unresolved. The possibility of multiple successor claims must not duplicate a resource, obligation, or decision power.

### 2.3 Separate transformations that often coincide

The following distinctions are analytical proposals for the laboratory, not an approved lifecycle taxonomy.

| Transformation | Evidence to examine | Inference that must not be automatic |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary participation change | Entry, departure, replacement, role transfer, continued backing | New participant set means new household |
| Property partition | Changes in claim holders, use, benefit, allocation rights | Division of assets means social fission |
| Fission | Sustained separation of organizing arrangements or decision processes | All cross-boundary relations must disappear |
| Succession | Transmission of responsibilities, claims, or recognized continuity | Largest successor or former leader's group necessarily continues |
| Consolidation | Newly sustained coordination among formerly distinct arrangements | Pooling resources once creates one household |
| Dissolution | Termination or loss of the relevant sustaining configuration | Temporary inactivity or missing observations proves termination |
| Reconsolidation | New coordination plus the intervening history of earlier arrangements | Similar final people/assets resurrect an earlier identity |

One history may contain several transformations. Property can divide before social fission, after it, or without it. One branch can continue an earlier process while another develops separately. The observer may detect a transition only later. A single “merged” or “split” flag is therefore a potentially lossy summary rather than an adequate substitute for the history.

### 2.4 Non-exclusive relationships require role-specific accounting

An individual can provide support to one configuration, reside with another, work under a third arrangement, and hold a claim elsewhere. These ties need not have the same boundary. A useful household representation must not generate rights or duties by silently inheriting them from a broad association. [P1]

Overlapping ties and competing interpretations are also different. Two support duties can both be real. Two incompatible hypotheses about who controls the same exclusive allocation cannot both be treated as valid authority merely because an inference method retains them. Similarly, a mixed-membership coefficient describes participation in an inferential model; it does not specify a fraction of a person's labor or property available to a collective. Airoldi and colleagues provide a concrete mixed-membership model whose node-level proportions concern interaction roles, not material entitlements. [S04]

Joint relations may require more than a list of person-to-person links. An agreement among A, B, and C is not necessarily equivalent to three independent bilateral agreements. X6 constructs precisely that ambiguity. The requirement is to preserve participants, roles, terms, and relation identity where they matter—not to select a graph database or insist that every simulation interaction be higher-order. [X6]

### 2.5 What state can belong to a household?

The useful test is not “is this field stored on a household object?” It is **what fact makes the state valid, who can change it, and what would invalidate it?** The following allocation is advisory.

| State | Defensible treatment | Failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Material stock, location, condition | Resource state; collective view may summarize | Copying stock into every associated household |
| Ownership, use, support or service claim | Particular claim/relation with holders and terms | Deriving ownership or control from residence or association |
| Personal preference, knowledge, fatigue | Individual state | Replacing personal states with a group-average mind |
| Coordinated plan and commitments | Persistent collective process, with contributors, backing and execution history | A group goal creating cooperation without an explanatory mechanism |
| Shared resource reservation | Commitment against identified resources and authority | A household balance that bypasses contested claims |
| Delegated responsibility | Explicitly supported role or mandate; scope and termination matter | Unlimited control by a designated “head” |
| Collective history or continuity assessment | Historical record plus interpretation/provenance | A display label becoming proof of its own continuity |
| Reputation or attributed identity | Beliefs/recognition of particular observers, with any summary clearly derived | An unexplained universal reputation variable |
| Household-level statistics | Recomputable views with defined population/rights scope | Summing overlapping ties as though all were exclusive membership |

Some of this state is genuinely historical: a plan can remain pending although the originating interaction has ended. Its causal persistence does not require an unexplained collective mind. The commitment, who is bound by it, and the means of performance can make its future effects intelligible. A collective representation may expose that state without being its independent source of authority.

Dependency or conflict must remain expressible. Shared plans need not imply equal bargaining power, complete agreement, or benevolent intent. Joint-action methods are useful for modeling particular cooperative processes; they are not a warrant to model every household as a unanimously consenting team.

### 2.6 Collective feedback has at least three different problems

**Execution circularity:** an individual's action depends on collective state while that collective state is being computed from the same actions. A candidate can resolve proposals against an explicit prior state, collect influences, determine compatible effects, and then update the resulting state. Influence–reaction models provide an established technical precedent for separating influences from state resolution, including multilevel interactions. They do not supply household semantics. [S05]

An illustrative, non-binding sequence is:

`lower-level facts → candidate/continuity evidence → backed plan or constraint → individual proposals → compatible realized effects → new lower-level facts`

This can be implemented with events, phases, or other execution models. It is not a decision for a global timestep. Same-time negotiation may instead require iteration, with an explicit termination rule and a treatment of multiple solutions.

**Epistemic circularity:** the detector creates a household, its controller encourages cooperation, and the resulting cooperation is counted as independent proof that the original detection was correct. Lagged updates do not cure this. X5's equation has two self-consistent states, and lagging preserves dependence on the initial state. Track feedback-mediated observations separately from independent backing evidence; allow reassessment when the backing disappears. [X5]

**Double execution:** a collective action and its individual contributors all debit the same resource. A valid collective commitment should affect the same resource ledger or physical transition, exactly once per realized effect. X4 checks this narrow arithmetic. It also shows that valid commitments can change action feasibility without changing an individual's preferences or granting a label causal power. [X4]

An analytical label should normally be removable without changing behavior. An identity recognized by people may legitimately matter if those people form beliefs and act on them. The latter effect must travel through explicit recognition, memory, expectations, norms, or relationships. The label-ablation test is not a prohibition on socially consequential identity; it distinguishes such a mechanism from accidental software authority.

### 2.7 Uncertainty is not one number

There are at least four independent questions: whether relevant relationships are observed; whether a pattern qualifies as household-scale organization under the current interpretation; whether it continues an earlier identity; and whether people within the world agree about that identity. Current activity is another dimension. A household candidate can be inactive but historically continuous, or actively coordinating while continuity remains contested.

A small laboratory can expose alternatives and supporting/conflicting evidence without assigning invented probabilities. A score of 0.7 is not a 70% probability unless its probabilistic model and calibration justify that interpretation. Nor does low observation confidence mean a real obligation is only partially enforceable.

Where action must proceed despite interpretive ambiguity, use independently established claims and commitments first. Exclusive disputed authority requires an explicit conflict or decision process; it cannot be granted to every retained hypothesis. Deferral, a norm-specific fallback, or separate counterfactual simulation branches are possible experiments. No universal “conservative” operational rule is adopted here.

## 3. Credible approaches discovered

### 3.1 Typed temporal relations with evolving-group recognition

Palla, Barabási, and Vicsek demonstrate tracking overlapping communities through time. That establishes a relevant computational family, not a household definition. Their empirical network domains and group-size findings should not be imported as household assumptions. [S01]

A project-specific candidate would retain distinguishable relationship types and inspect recurring configurations. A recognition method could identify candidate boundaries; another mechanism would consider continuity and collective capacity. This preserves discovery rather than prescribing every household in advance.

The central risk is semantic substitution: a network objective measures a structural property, which is then renamed “household.” Distinct ties can have opposite implications for organization, and a shared participant can connect otherwise separate configurations. Components, density, centrality, and overlap are useful baselines precisely because their failures are inspectable. They are not sufficient foundations merely because they produce convenient groups. [X1, X6]

Standard global modularity also has a known resolution limitation dependent on network-wide scale. X8 reproduces a specific local preference reversal after adding a disconnected component. This is a warning about that objective, not all network methods. [S10, X8]

### 3.2 Temporal lineage and multifaceted change descriptions

Failla and colleagues describe group changes through continuous event facets and archetype weights rather than forcing each change into one exclusive category. Their method takes temporally ordered partitions as input; its “identity” facet concerns member transfer. It is not a direct model of persistent household identity, overlapping obligations, or action rights. [S02]

The transferable idea is to separate observations of change from labels such as fission or consolidation. A household candidate could retain evidence about dispersed participants, transmitted obligations, interrupted coordination, and successor claims before assigning a lifecycle interpretation. These proposed additional dimensions are this report's extrapolation, not implemented components of Failla's method.

Lineage can accompany either network-first or process-first recognition. It must retain the difference between “derived from” and “is the same as.” A mere chain of matched snapshots still risks the drift observed in X2. A record saying that a new configuration succeeded an earlier one should identify its warrant, not only its similarity score.

### 3.3 Generative and mixed-membership inference

Peixoto develops generative inference for layered, edge-valued and time-varying networks, including model selection. Airoldi and colleagues supply mixed-membership models permitting different roles across interactions. These are credible sources for probabilistic and overlapping recognition, although neither provides the historical semantics or collective-action mechanism required here. [S03, S04]

A candidate could infer several organizational configurations from typed observations and retain uncertainty rather than force an immediate assignment. Persistence could be represented through a separate temporal model, with lineage hypotheses distinguished from ordinary role overlap.

This becomes expensive conceptually before it becomes expensive computationally. What is observed, what is latent, why observations support organization, and what changes represent genuine social change rather than observation error must all be defined. A posterior is conditional on those modeling choices. Adding inference sophistication to fixtures with no defensible observation model would create numerical precision without clearer household semantics.

For the first laboratory, explicit alternative explanations may offer more diagnostic value than a large latent model. Probabilistic inference deserves a separate candidate only if uncertainty changes an important conclusion or action and simpler evidence tracking cannot express the problem adequately. This is an implementation-economy recommendation, not a finding that Bayesian approaches are intrinsically unsuitable.

### 3.4 Process and commitment continuity, with optional collective reification

Levesque, Cohen, and Nunes distinguish acting together from coincident individual action using joint commitments and intentions. Their formal account helps identify persistent coordination obligations; it does not demonstrate automatic household discovery or supply a general model of all dependency relationships. [S06]

A process-first candidate recognizes recurring arrangements in the fabric: commitments accepted or imposed through modeled relations, coordination of identified resources, substitution of participants, and obligations surviving particular episodes. It creates a durable reference to the resulting configuration only when there is a basis for doing so. A later household representation may expose these commitments, history, and available collective actions.

This is attractive where the task's primary challenge is explaining why an organization continues and acts. It makes a change of residence or coordinator relatively unproblematic when the sustaining arrangements remain. It also identifies when a former organizing process has actually terminated, rather than merely lost a statistical majority of members.

Its weakness is possible overformalization. If every ordinary social tie must become a consciously agreed contract, the model has changed the problem. Informal reciprocity, asymmetry, dependency, conflicting understandings, and coordination without shared beliefs need independent representation. A single recurring process is also not necessarily a household. The recognizer still needs to distinguish a household-scale configuration from a temporary task group.

### 3.5 Event logic, histories, and grounded explanations

Kowalski and Sergot's Event Calculus reasons about events and the periods during which conditions hold. Fowler's event-sourcing account describes recovering state from recorded changes, including the problem of replaying side effects. PROV-DM supplies concepts for entities, activities, derivations, agents and responsibility. These support historical explanation through different technical routes; they are not interchangeable architectures. [S07–S09]

For this project, their common value is preserving why something currently holds and how that explanation changes. A renewal, fulfillment, transfer, revocation, or observation correction should not be confused with deleting the fact that an earlier event happened. X7 illustrates this distinction for the validity of a current plan's backing.

Several implementations remain possible: current-state records with sufficient change history; a temporal logic layer; event replay; or a combination. Full event sourcing is not required by the accepted foundation. Neither is a specific provenance standard. The essential question is whether the chosen route can answer the required causal and continuity queries without inventing missing history.

A lifecycle state machine alone is inadequate if its transitions are directly scripted household outcomes. It becomes useful when it summarizes independently established lower-level changes and permits unresolved or contested interpretations. Replay must also retain the interpretation/rule version used: a new recognizer can produce a different retrospective classification without changing what originally happened.

### 3.6 N-ary relations and incidence representations

Higher-order interaction models represent interactions among groups rather than only pairs; Alvarez-Rodriguez and colleagues provide one such research example. This is a representational option, not a reason to model every relation as a hyperedge. [S11]

X6 establishes a narrower requirement: an untyped pair projection can discard whether three people share one agreement or instead have three bilateral agreements. A relation record with identified participants preserves the distinction. It can be implemented as a hyperedge, an ordinary graph with relation nodes, a relational record with participant associations, or another lossless form.

A June 2026 arXiv version by Llabrés and colleagues reports exact reductions for a class of higher-order social-impact models, with weights that may depend on configuration. This cautions against claiming that all higher-order dynamics are irreducible. It does not justify discarding the identity or terms of joint obligations. Only that manuscript's abstract and metadata were inspected. [S12]

### 3.7 Composite candidates rather than a catalogue winner

The literature families address different responsibilities. Three serious combinations merit comparison:

**Candidate A — Relation-first, evidence-backed continuity.** Typed relations generate organizational candidates. A distinct historical assessment tracks their continuity and lineage. Collective effects require supported commitments or capacities, not cluster membership. This retains open-ended discovery, but the interpretation layer can become a collection of hidden ad hoc rules unless explanations and negative controls remain central.

**Candidate B — Process-first, historically grounded collective.** Recurring coordination and obligations constitute a persistent configuration. A collective reference indexes its history and backed capabilities; participants and resources can change. Relation-based descriptions remain available without controlling identity. This foregrounds agency and succession, but risks mistaking explicit processes for all meaningful household organization.

**Candidate C — Uncertainty-bearing recognition plus lineage.** Several candidate configurations or continuities remain live under an explicit observation/inference model. Independently supported claims govern action while interpretations are unresolved. This expresses genuine ambiguity well, but can obscure semantics and multiply histories before basic phenomena are understood.

A and B may eventually converge. Keeping them distinct during validation is useful: it reveals whether the recognizer discovers meaningful organization beyond explicit processes, and whether those processes explain persistence better than structural matching. C should be added where it resolves a demonstrated ambiguity rather than as a default sophistication layer.

## 4. Comparison

The judgments below are task-specific engineering analysis. They are not benchmark rankings or measured runtime claims. “Complexity” concerns what the laboratory would need to explain and maintain, not a universal ranking of the source methods.

| Approach | Main contribution | Missing responsibility / principal risk | Complexity and scale | Observability | Migration and reversibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Typed relation recognition | Discovers configurations without preassigned households | Structural criterion can substitute for social organization | Local pattern rules may be cheap; enumerating all subsets is not | Good when relation selection and rule contributions are explicit | Relatively reversible if source relations survive; hard if only inferred labels are kept |
| Global graph partition objective | Compact grouping and a convenient baseline | Exclusive partitions, global coupling, semantic gap | Population-wide inference can couple unrelated changes | Objective inspectable; household meaning often weak | High risk if partition IDs become authoritative social facts |
| Lineage / multifaceted change | Separates continuity and transformation descriptions | Resemblance or member flow alone does not establish succession | Candidate matching and branching histories can grow | Good with per-link warrants and rule versions | Strong if descriptive evidence is retained separately from verdicts |
| Process / commitment continuity | Connects persistence to actual organization and action | Overformalization; temporary teams mistaken for households | Local process updates can be manageable; negotiation/conflict can dominate | Strong for explicit commitments; weak for omitted informal ties | Process records reusable, but hard-coded norms can be costly to remove |
| Probabilistic / mixed-membership inference | Alternative configurations and quantified model uncertainty | Conditional inference mistaken for truth or entitlement | More model-selection, inference and calibration work; cost depends on model | Requires exposing assumptions and competing hypotheses, not just probabilities | Latent-label churn is manageable only if it does not rewrite primitive identities |
| Event logic / historical derivation | Explains validity, succession evidence and replay | Does not determine which configurations are households | Full replay grows with history; indexed dependencies can localize work | Strong with complete event and derivation records | Good if raw facts, interpretation versions and external effects remain separable |
| N-ary / incidence representation | Preserves joint terms and overlapping role structures | Representation alone supplies neither recognition nor agency | Cost tied to relation arity and participation; rich matching can grow | Good with explicit participants and roles | Lossless encodings can migrate; flattened untyped projections may be irreversible |

### 4.1 Scaling traps worth detecting now

For `n` individuals, unrestricted enumeration of all candidate subsets considers `2^n` subsets. This is a combinatorial count, not an asserted runtime of a particular method. Candidate generation therefore needs a defensible locality or relevance mechanism if it ever moves beyond tiny fixtures. Imposing a fixed household size solely to avoid that cost would substitute a computational shortcut for an unresolved modeling decision.

A temporal method may generate many competing correspondences among candidate configurations. Retaining every branching interpretation indefinitely can produce a different scale problem from finding configurations at one time. Pruning hypotheses without keeping the reason for pruning makes later reconsolidation hard to interpret. A small lab can expose this explicitly before selecting retention limits.

Cached household summaries have a quieter risk: a revoked underlying claim may cease to be valid while a derived capability survives. Dependency invalidation and fresh execution checks matter more initially than reducing a few lookups. Local updates must still follow genuine long-range dependencies; “local” must not mean ignoring a remote support duty or shared resource.

The global-modularity probe is particularly revealing. Adding an unrelated disconnected clique changed which of two partitions of the original local configuration had the higher objective. A household identity rule should face this perturbation test; otherwise expanding the simulated world may rewrite old households without any local social event. A mechanism with a justified global dependence can explain that dependence, but it should not acquire it accidentally from an optimization objective. [X8]

### 4.2 Preserve reversibility by limiting what conclusions can change

An uncertain recognition should be revisable without rewriting the existence of a person, resource, or original obligation. A continuity reassessment may alter the interpreted lineage, but should leave a trace of the earlier assessment if it affected simulated decisions. An analytical alternative that was never acted upon need not become an in-world event.

The most expensive irreversible move would be making every right, resource, and personal relation subordinate to an inferred household ID. That would make changing the recognizer change the world itself. The accepted distinction between the social fabric and emergent collectives offers a way to avoid that coupling. [P1]

## 5. Worked social-fabric examples

These are deliberately synthetic histories. They assert no ancient frequencies, inheritance rules, residence conventions, or decision norms. Letters denote people, not fixed members; `H` denotes an observer's reference to a possible historical household. The treatments below describe candidate behavior to test, not implemented outcomes of complete literature algorithms.

### 5.1 Property partition with continued household identity

**Initial configuration.** A–F participate in recurring resource coordination. Particular claims govern use of a field and stores. Some support obligations survive individual absence. The arrangement has an ongoing plan whose contributors can be replaced. Its identity is not stipulated merely from six people living together.

**Lower-level change.** Claim holders divide property into two bundles. D–F move elsewhere. Nothing in this change by itself terminates the recurring coordination, outstanding support, or accepted responsibilities. People continue fulfilling the existing arrangement across residences. A subsequent participant replacement or coordinator departure is recorded separately.

**Candidate A: relation-first.** A detector examining residence or ownership alone produces two groups. One examining the relevant coordinating relationships retains evidence for one configuration. The continuity assessment asks whether the former organization remained supported, not whether all edges, places or people stayed unchanged. A history-backed explanation would say that the property and residence changes occurred while the organizing arrangements continued. Kinship is neither necessary nor sufficient for that conclusion.

**Candidate B: process-first.** Existing coordinated processes continue through the claim changes, with allocations recalculated under the new rights. The persistent collective reference remains supported if its constitutive processes and continuity warrants remain in force. Replacement of a contributor is an event affecting a role or obligation, not automatic creation of a new household. A broken property claim must not be concealed by a surviving household label.

**Candidate C: uncertainty-bearing.** The observer can retain “continuing organization across two places” and “emerging separation” as alternatives if future coordination is unclear. Known support or use rights remain operative according to their own validity. The observer does not split those rights probabilistically merely because household recognition is uncertain.

**Discriminating variation.** Change only continued coordination: after partition, recurring joint allocations cease and separate arrangements begin. A detector that always preserves identity after property division now fails in the opposite direction. Another variation leaves only occasional gifts across the residences, testing whether weak support is being treated as proof of a single household.

**Executed result.** X1's partition fixture retains one coordination component while residence/property proxies each produce two groups. This is the intended counterexample to automatic equivalence; it does not prove that one connected coordination component always constitutes a household. The continuing-identity interpretation in this worked narrative additionally depends on the explicitly described supporting history. [X1]

### 5.2 Genuine fission with continuing kinship/support

**Initial configuration.** The same recurring arrangement links A–F. A later sequence withdraws common allocation responsibilities, ends its encompassing plan, and establishes two separately sustained arrangements: A–C and D–F. These are lower-level changes; no fixture commands the software to split a household.

**Residual ties.** A continues supporting F, and C and D remain related. A field may even remain subject to claims on both sides. Those links are represented rather than deleted for visual neatness. Each emerging arrangement nevertheless organizes its own ongoing activity and can revise its plans independently within the remaining obligations.

**Candidate A: relation-first.** An untyped connectivity rule keeps all six people in one component. A role-sensitive recognizer sees evidence of two organizing patterns and distinguishes them from inter-pattern support. Its historical component assesses whether one is a continuation, both are descendants, or continuity is disputed. It must not choose the largest branch without examining the relevant evidence.

**Candidate B: process-first.** The encompassing arrangement terminates or changes into separate processes. Each new configuration can acquire its own persistent reference once supported. A support duty across the boundary remains attached to its actual parties and terms. If one branch continues a previous responsibility or recognized identity, that is recorded as a particular warrant, not a universal succession rule. A–C and D–F do not each inherit the entire old stock or every former mandate.

**Candidate C: uncertainty-bearing.** During gradual separation, the model can retain a transitional interpretation rather than flicker between one and two households. Evidence for distinct coordination and evidence for continuing ties are shown separately. Mixed organizational features do not require immediately choosing one pure lifecycle category. The loss of a shared plan is not automatically inferred from a missing observation of it.

**Asymmetry and contestation.** Replace the equal split with four people on one side and two on the other. X2 gives Jaccard scores of 2/3 and 1/3, respectively. That is only an overlap calculation. The smaller branch might retain an explicit continuity warrant; alternatively neither might, or both might claim succession. The model needs room for these cases rather than identifying size with historical priority. [X2]

**Executed result.** X1 produces two components in the coordination layer and one in the untyped union. X6 separately shows that a person participating in two relation sets collapses them into one component under projection. Together they establish that cross-boundary ties and overlap cannot simply be erased or treated as automatic unity. Neither probe discovers fission without supplied relation changes. [X1, X6]

### 5.3 Later reconsolidation: continuation, new organization, or unresolved

**Common endpoint.** A–F again coordinate the same resources at the same place. To a detector receiving only those current facts, the configurations look identical. Three intervening histories make different evidence available.

**History R1 — preserved backing.** During separation, some relevant organizing obligations remained active, or were explicitly renewed or transmitted. Joint activity later resumes through that chain. This supports a continuity interpretation, subject to what the project's household criterion considers constitutive. It need not entail that every temporary arrangement disappeared.

**History R2 — interruption and new formation.** The relevant previous obligations were explicitly released or fulfilled, the encompassing arrangement ended, and a later set of new commitments recreates similar cooperation. This supports distinguishing the new organization from the old despite similar participants and assets. A project's eventual concept might still allow socially recognized revival, but that would require evidence and an explicit interpretation—not automatic equality from resemblance.

**History R3 — insufficient observation or disagreement.** Activity ceased to be observed, but no complete record establishes what happened to sustaining duties or recognition. Alternatively, some participants acknowledge revival while others describe a new arrangement. The proper research output is unresolved evidence or contested recognition, not a randomly chosen binary answer.

**Candidate A: relation-first.** The snapshot recognizer can identify a present candidate in all three histories. Only a separate continuity assessment distinguishes them. Reusing an old identifier because the current configuration matches its old signature would fail R2 and conceal R3. A history-bearing signature is possible, but then continuity information is already part of the mechanism rather than recovered from resemblance.

**Candidate B: process-first.** The system checks continuity paths across particular obligations/processes and records which terminated. Resumption under preserved backing differs from a new process after release. However, process identity cannot simply be substituted for household identity: perhaps one small obligation survived while the wider organization ended. The interpretation must state why the preserved element matters rather than treating any surviving edge as sufficient.

**Candidate C: uncertainty-bearing.** The competing interpretations attach to lineage, not to the existence of the current people or stock. R3 can remain unresolved while current jointly backed actions proceed. Where disputed succession affects an exclusive right, the candidate needs a modeled dispute or decision process. A high similarity posterior must not authorize both claimants to spend the same resource.

**Executed result.** X3 replays a renewal history and a termination/recreation history. Their terminal projections, excluding origins, hash identically; their continuity evidence differs. The unobserved-gap control reports an unknown continuity interval. This demonstrates an information requirement, not a universal rule that one renewed agreement proves household revival. [X3]

### 5.4 Collective action across these histories

Assume a supported coordinated plan reserves six units from a stock of ten; an individual requests six. Under the illustrative constraints, four units remain available to that request. Revoking the plan's backing increases availability to six. Renaming or removing the observer's analytical household label leaves availability at four while the commitment remains valid. [X4]

This is a concrete route from collective state to individual possibilities. The plan is maintained historically and constrains action, but its force derives from backing relationships and identified resources. The example assumes the plan and its validity; a future closed-loop prototype must explain how participants or other modeled relations produce and maintain it.

Overlapping participation creates a further execution test. Requests for eight, five, and three units against ten yield five distinct sequential allocations across six request orders. An illustrative proportional resolver produces the same allocation in every order: five, 25/8 and 15/8. That is not a recommended household allocation norm. It demonstrates that ordering is a causal choice: the laboratory should represent meaningful priority, negotiation, or simultaneous resolution rather than allow incidental collection order to decide it. ## 6. Failure modes / falsification conditions

A candidate should be challenged by the following conditions. “Failure” means the mechanism contradicts the stated test assumptions or cannot explain its output, not that one interpretation is universally wrong in history.

| Failure condition | Why it matters | Probe or next test |
|---|---|---|
| Property division alone creates social fission | Collapses separable claims and organization | X1; varied claim-only perturbations |
| A cross-boundary support tie forces two arrangements into one household | Makes residual ties incompatible with fission | X1, X6 |
| A changing address, asset bundle, or coordinator automatically ends identity | Makes a particular constituent the hidden essence | Worked case 5.1; turnover/relocation controls |
| High adjacent overlap preserves identity without any substantive warrant | Allows unbounded drift | X2; total-turnover histories with/without transmitted backing |

## 7. Recommended next validation

### 7.1 Compare two minimal closed-loop candidates

Build Candidate A and Candidate B over the same lower-level fixtures. Keep motives deliberately minimal and explicitly provisional: each person can choose among supported actions affecting a small number of resources and obligations. The comparison must not encode a desired household outcome as a preferred choice. Use the same request/offer opportunities and exogenous disturbances for both candidates.

The experimental event vocabulary may include an accepted contribution, a withdrawn obligation, a transfer of a specific responsibility, a resource change, an arrival or departure, and an observation gap where appropriate. It should not include a test-driver instruction that directly forms, splits, or merges a household. A derived report may describe such transitions after the underlying changes occur.

The first question is whether the two candidates produce different explanations from the same lower-level history. The second, tested separately, is whether their operational representations alter subsequent behavior in justified ways. Mixing these stages immediately would make it difficult to distinguish a recognition difference from feedback created by that difference.

### 7.2 Use crossed controls, not one narrative per desired result

Cross property partition with continued versus discontinued coordination; relocation with preserved versus ended obligations; participant turnover with transmitted versus untransmitted responsibilities; and residual support with one versus two ongoing decision arrangements. Include reconsolidation after explicit release, after continued backing, and after missing observations. Add the temporary-team negative control and overlapping participation with shared resources.

Each fixture should state what is fixed, what changes, which conclusions are warranted by construction, and which remain genuinely ambiguous. Where household identity is deliberately underdetermined, success means preserving that uncertainty and its consequences, not guessing a reference label. Counterfactual pairs are especially valuable: change one relevant feature while preserving the endpoint or the material setting.

### 7.3 Measure diagnostic properties

Record transition timing relative to lower-level events, unwarranted identity changes under irrelevant perturbations, missed meaningful transitions under stated fixture semantics, and preservation of simultaneous non-exclusive ties. Record the fraction of realized collective effects with a complete backing explanation and the number of resource/authority conflicts left unresolved or incorrectly duplicated. These are proposed diagnostic measures, not empirical rates produced by this return.

Test identifier permutation and input-order changes. Equivalent labels should not change results unless modeled actor recognition makes the label meaningful. Where simultaneous actions have multiple valid outcomes, the test should require a disclosed resolver and reproducibility under its stated assumptions, not universal order invariance.

For genuine probabilistic candidates, evaluate uncertainty under known synthetic data-generating assumptions and separately test robustness to misspecification. Do not calibrate a recognizer against labels generated by the same household rule and then report that as independent validation. Facet scores, confidence heuristics, actor disagreement, and posterior probabilities need separate reporting.

### 7.4 Require causal ablations and explainability tests

Remove only the analytical label; retain all agreements. Remove only a backing commitment; retain the label. Interrupt a process but preserve the final participant set. Add an unrelated disconnected configuration. Revoke a shared claim after a summary has been cached. Compare the resulting action and identity traces.

An explanation should identify the underlying events or currently valid conditions, the interpretation rule and its version, the alternatives considered, and the effect on action. It should also answer a contrastive question such as: “Why did this property partition not count as fission?” A narrative generated after the fact without those dependencies is not sufficient evidence of causal observability.

### 7.5 Defer heavy inference until it has a demonstrated job

Candidate C should enter the comparison when ambiguous recognition materially affects a result that A and B cannot represent cleanly. A small set of explicit alternatives may be adequate initially. If richer inference is introduced, measure growth in candidate configurations, continuity hypotheses, retained history, and dependency re-evaluations rather than promising scalability from nominal algorithm complexity alone.

A useful stopping condition for this validation stage is not agreement on every ambiguous household. It is the ability to distinguish the required cases under explicit assumptions, retain uncertainty where those assumptions do not settle identity, and explain any collective effect without relying on an ungrounded container. The Master Architect can then choose among remaining tradeoffs with evidence.

## 9. Unresolved questions and assumptions

**Household recognition remains semantically open.** Which recurring arrangements distinguish household-scale organization from other collaboration? How much informality, dependency, and disagreement must the first experiment represent? Neither network structure nor formal joint commitment settles this from the supplied packet.

**Continuity conventions remain open.** Which transmissions warrant persistence, when may a historical identity be revived, and how should divergent successor claims be represented? These are not resolved by overlap, resource ownership, or the identity of a central person. The proposed lineage distinctions make the question inspectable rather than deciding it.

**The status of observation remains open.** The simulation may know all lower-level facts while participants know less; the recognizer may also be an analytical observer with incomplete inputs. Unknown evidence, disputed recognition, and absent world facts must not be conflated. X3's gap branch concerns the observer's uncertainty, not a claim that canonical simulation events are missing.

**Collective-action mechanisms remain open.** How are plans formed, contested, transmitted, abandoned and executed? Which actions rely on consensus, delegation, dependency or another modeled relation? The reservation and proportional-allocation probes assume specific test rules; they do not recommend historical household governance or allocation norms.

**Implementation choices remain open.** No language, engine, database, graph representation, temporal logic system, scheduling model, threshold, probabilistic model, UI or production population target is selected. The Python code is a reproducible research instrument only.

**Advisory conclusion.** Preserve the distinction between constitutive evidence, historical continuity, and backed collective action. Compare relation-first and process-first candidates on the same closed-loop adversarial histories. Introduce richer inference only where uncertainty demands it. A persistent household reference can be useful; what matters is whether every claimed continuity and every exercised capacity remains accountable to the social fabric rather than to the existence of that reference.
