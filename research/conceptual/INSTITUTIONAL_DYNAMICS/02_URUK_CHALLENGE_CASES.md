# Uruk-Derived Challenge Cases

**Status:** DRAFT / NON-AUTHORITATIVE  
**Role:** Future falsification/design cases. These are questions the eventual mechanism should survive, not scripted outcomes.

## 1. The Fork

Start from one recognized collective with one causal history.

Create two successor formations that each preserve substantial people, records, procedures, authority claims, and name continuity.

Questions:

- when does one institution become two;
- can both claim continuity;
- what is objective predecessor relation;
- what do insiders and outsiders recognize;
- does one branch inherit more official authority without being the only causal descendant.

Failure mode exposed: assuming one stable ID or majority-overlap metric can settle institutional descent.

## 2. The Usurpation

Two people plausibly claim the same office.

Different participant/outsider populations recognize different claimants.

Questions:

- what is the objective occupancy/appointment state;
- what authority claims exist;
- which actions are accepted by whom;
- can operational capability move toward one claimant before legitimacy converges;
- how does later settlement rewrite—or fail to rewrite—remembered history.

Failure mode exposed: collapsing authority, capability, and recognition.

## 3. Migration / Relocation

Move some combination of:

- people;
- office-holders;
- archives;
- resources;
- ritual/procedural artifacts;
- name;
- physical place.

Questions:

- which continuity channels matter for this institution;
- can identity persist without place;
- can an abandoned site retain symbolic/official claims while the active institution moved;
- can two competing continuities emerge.

Failure mode exposed: treating location or assets as identity.

## 4. The Empty Shell

Preserve name, place, archive, and perhaps legal claims while purpose, procedures, participants, and authority network change substantially.

Questions:

- when is continuity merely official;
- can objective numerical identity persist despite radical institutional transformation;
- should the engine force dissolution, or can later historians/actors contest the meaning.

Failure mode exposed: forcing metaphysical identity from surface continuity.

## 5. The Lost Archive

Destroy or make inaccessible major records while living participants, routines, and offices remain.

Questions:

- what continuity survives through people and practice;
- what institutional memory is lost;
- how do later participants reconstruct origin;
- can official genealogy become less accurate while the institution remains operational.

Failure mode exposed: equating archive survival with institutional survival.

## 6. The Returning Predecessor

A former legitimate office-holder returns after lawful or effective succession.

Questions:

- historical legitimacy versus current occupancy;
- actor-specific recognition;
- residual influence;
- claims based on former authority;
- whether procedures or tradition permit restoration.

Failure mode exposed: treating prior legitimacy as current authority.

## 7. The False Founder

An institution's official records attribute founding to a person/event different from the simulator's causal history.

Questions:

- how the official story became authoritative;
- whether actors know contradictory evidence;
- whether correction changes legitimacy, identity, or only narrative;
- whether outsiders reconstruct the causal genealogy.

Failure mode exposed: collapsing official narrative into objective history.

## 8. The Foreign Observer

Multiple outsiders observe different partial evidence about the same institution.

Questions:

- which identity/authority claims each recognizes;
- how trade/diplomacy/cooperation depends on subjective institutional models;
- how later information changes recognition;
- whether foreign recognition affects practical capability without defining objective continuity.

Failure mode exposed: global omniscience.

## 9. Companion-case extensions

### The Anonymous Artist

A contributor's work is institutionalized while attribution disappears.

Test:

- causal contribution persists;
- artifact/procedure survives;
- remembered genealogy loses the contributor;
- later actors remain causally constrained by the anonymous contribution.

### The Two-Ledger Integrator

Responsibility and accumulated knowledge make a formally junior actor an effective organizational bottleneck.

Test:

- formal office remains unchanged;
- effective centrality grows through dependency/knowledge;
- procedures externalize some knowledge;
- institutional capability can improve while individual burden worsens.

### The Unappointed Jester

Repeated interaction creates a recognizable informal social function without a formal office.

Test:

- influence emerges from relationship/access/history;
- no formal authority is granted;
- actor can affect institutional interpretation indirectly;
- role may disappear with the holder or later become formalized.

## 10. Use

A future design candidate should not be accepted merely because it handles its happy path.

Select challenge cases appropriate to its claimed scope and require the model to explain:

- objective state;
- causal history;
- actor-specific knowledge/recognition;
- authority;
- capability;
- resulting actions;
- residual ambiguity.

The challenge cases should expose where a proposed universal rule destroys distinctions that the project needs to preserve.
