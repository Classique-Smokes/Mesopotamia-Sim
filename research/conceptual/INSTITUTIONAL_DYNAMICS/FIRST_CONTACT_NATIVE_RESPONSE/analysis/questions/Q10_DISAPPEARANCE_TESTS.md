# Q10 — Two Disappearance Tests

**Surface coverage:** INDIRECT / TRANSFORMED  
**Analytical stage:** native extraction only; no Uruk comparison

## Delivered question

The tablet asked two culture-persistence counterfactuals:

A. Conservatory culture/history documents disappear while people and agents keep behaving similarly. Does the Conservatory still exist?

B. Conservatory documents survive perfectly while future contributors stop behaving according to them and stop recognizing the roles. Does the Conservatory still exist?

## How the response transforms the problem

The handoff does **not** test documents-versus-practice directly.

Instead, section 13 asks what happens if three kinds of actors disappear:

1. implementation AI;
2. high-context Threadkeeper;
3. Game Director.

It then asks what can be reconstructed from the repository alone.

## Native material

### Implementation AI disappearance

- **p.60:** loss would be manageable.
- A successor could reconstruct most current technical state from source code, CURRENT_STATE, UnityProjectContext, Validation, playtest docs, Git history, and tests.

### Threadkeeper disappearance

- **p.61:** more nuance would be lost, but much less than before continuity work.
- Harder-to-reconstruct material includes relationships among distant design discussions, intensity of preferences, discarded alternatives, subtle meanings of project vocabulary, and why a technically valid compromise felt dangerous.
- DESIGN_RATIONALE protects some of this.

### Game Director disappearance

- **pp.61–62:** this is called the most severe continuity loss.
- The repository can preserve decisions and reasoning but cannot authoritatively decide whether movement feels alive, UI feels inviting, a compromise belongs aesthetically, or the game still expresses the intended emotional center.
- The project intentionally preserves this human authority rather than pretending to eliminate it.

### Repository-only reconstruction

- **pp.62–63:** a successor can reconstruct active milestone, runtime architecture, save format, test state, most locked design pillars, historical milestone sequence, known limitations, agent constraints, and recent continuity additions.
- Difficult material includes uncommitted local state, unwritten current human judgment, unexternalized rationale, recent conversational alternatives, and unwritten priorities.

## Native-position reconstruction

The response's persistence model is **actor-loss and reconstructability oriented**, rather than culture-document-versus-practice oriented.

It distinguishes:

- technical continuity that can survive disposable implementers;
- semantic/rationale continuity that is partly externalized but still loses nuance with the Threadkeeper;
- creative/experiential authority that remains specifically human and is not reconstructed merely by preserving documents.

This is a meaningful native transformation of the delivered counterfactual, but it does not settle whether "the Conservatory" survives when documents and lived recognition diverge.

## What remains unanswered

The artifact does not say:

- whether Conservatory identity can persist with no culture documents if practice remains;
- whether perfect documents without lived practice amount to a dead archive, dormant institution, or still-existing Conservatory;
- whether recognition by participants is necessary for institutional persistence.

Those are genuine gaps.
