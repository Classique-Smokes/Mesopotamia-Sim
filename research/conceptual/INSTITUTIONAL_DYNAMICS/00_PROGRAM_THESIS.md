# Draft Program Thesis — Institutional Identity, Persistence, and Recognition

**Status:** DRAFT / NON-AUTHORITATIVE  
**Role:** Conceptual synthesis

## 1. Umbrella problem

The project already commits to higher-order structures becoming real causal actors without becoming detached abstractions.

The draft institutional-dynamics program asks what machinery would let that happen coherently over time.

The central question is:

> **How can a higher-order social entity remain historically intelligible as “the same thing,” act through changing people and offices, survive partial turnover and memory loss, be recognized differently by different actors, and sometimes divide or lose continuity—without identity being reduced to current composition, one scalar, or an engine fiat?**

This is broader than household identity and narrower than “simulate all institutions.”

## 2. Core draft distinctions

Future design should preserve these as separate dimensions unless evidence/design work later demonstrates a justified merger:

1. **Numerical / engine identity** — which persistent record is being referenced.
2. **Historical continuity** — what causal chain justifies treating successive states as one continuing entity.
3. **Operational state** — active, inactive, incapable, dissolved, vacant, etc.
4. **Authority** — who is entitled to act, in what scope, and by what basis.
5. **Capability** — what the entity can presently accomplish through available people, resources, procedures, commitments, and offices.
6. **Internal legitimacy / recognition** — which participants accept an authority/identity proposition.
7. **External recognition** — which outsiders recognize the entity, office, claimant, or authority.
8. **Effective influence** — actual causal/network centrality, which may diverge from formal office.
9. **Institutional memory / official genealogy** — what the entity's durable artifacts and practices claim about its own history.
10. **Actual causal genealogy** — what the simulator's objective event history says actually produced the present state.

No single scalar “institution strength,” “legitimacy,” “status,” or “continuity” should silently substitute for these distinctions.

## 3. Draft invariants worth testing

These are candidate design invariants, not accepted rules.

### Current similarity is not continuity

Two collectives with similar people, resources, place, or name are not automatically the same institution.

Conversely, meaningful continuity may survive large changes in visible composition.

### Engine identity is bookkeeping, not proof

A stable ID lets the engine refer to something. It does not by itself justify historical continuity.

### Higher-order action needs a grounded execution path

A statement such as “the temple lent grain” may be a legitimate macro-description only if the simulation can reconstruct a lower-level causal implementation through offices/actors, authority, resources, process, and commit/history.

### Recognition is causal but not constitutive by itself

Recognition may enable action, cooperation, obedience, exchange, or legitimacy. Recognition does not automatically create objective continuity.

### Authority, capability, and recognition may diverge

A legitimate office-holder may lack capability. A capable claimant may lack recognized authority. Outsiders and insiders may disagree.

### Causal genealogy and social genealogy may diverge

The simulator may retain one objective event lineage while actors, records, or institutions preserve different narratives.

### Higher-order persistence must not erase lower-order participants

Institutional continuity should constrain later people without making the people, offices, relationships, resources, and historical transitions irrelevant.

## 4. Externalized causality

The broad common mechanism suggested by the Uruk corpus is **externalized causality**:

1. an actor or group acts;
2. the action creates a durable arrangement, artifact, rule, office, obligation, procedure, reputation, or institutional relation;
3. later actors encounter that structure;
4. the structure changes their available actions, incentives, beliefs, recognition, or authority;
5. the original actors may be absent, dead, forgotten, or misremembered.

This is a candidate explanation for how institutions become causal without requiring a supernatural institutional mind.

## 5. Why this belongs after / above SFL v0

SFL v0 is the project's first test of the required substrate:

- persistent people and typed relationships;
- history-bearing transitions;
- actor-specific subjective state;
- household formation/continuity;
- representative role and succession;
- delayed processes / stable continuation.

If those mechanisms do not work coherently, a generalized institutional model would be premature.

If they do work, the household becomes the first empirical/reference case from which to discover which primitives generalize.
