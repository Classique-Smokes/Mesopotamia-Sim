# SFL v0 Discovery Seams — What the Remaining Slices May Teach Us

**Status:** DURABLE NON-AUTHORITATIVE / PROVISIONAL GUIDING FRAMEWORK  
**Role:** Slice-linked conceptual synthesis and future retrieval aid  
**Source basis:** Accepted SFL v0 architecture/specification + Institutional Dynamics guiding framework  
**Authority:** None. This document does not create simulation semantics, slice scope, acceptance requirements, or roadmap commitments.

## 1. Purpose

SFL v0 was designed before the Uruk Accident conceptual work was synthesized.

Nevertheless, the accepted five-slice progression now appears to expose a sequence of increasingly rich questions that overlap strongly with the later Institutional Dynamics framework.

This document preserves that observation so future work can **notice what the implemented machinery may already be teaching us** before inventing new institutional abstractions.

The central interpretive claim is deliberately modest:

> The remaining SFL v0 slices can be treated as controlled opportunities to observe smaller forms of problems that later institutional work may need to solve.

This is not evidence that the current household model generalizes to all institutions, and it is not permission to add Uruk-derived mechanics to v0.

## 2. Slice 1 — externalized causality and path dependence

Slice 1 supplies persistent people, typed relationships/arrangements, proposals/responses, committed effects, and semantic history.

The deeper question it begins to expose is:

> Can an action create durable social structure that changes later possibilities after the original interaction has ended?

Debt, favour, marriage, residence, and directed attitude are primitive examples.

An interaction can leave behind something that later affects:

- candidate generation;
- feasibility;
- obligations;
- counterpart choices;
- material access;
- future reactions.

This is a small form of **externalized causality**: past action remains active through durable state.

### Signals worth observing

During Birth Run 001 and later Slice-1 observations, look for:

- chains where one event changes the possibilities of a later event;
- relationships or obligations that create visible path dependence;
- recurring interaction patterns that arise from retained history rather than scripted sequencing;
- whether causal history is sufficient to explain why a later option existed or disappeared.

### Do not infer

Slice 1 cannot establish:

- institutional identity;
- legitimacy;
- actor-specific social knowledge;
- household emergence;
- office continuity;
- historical realism of the reference policy.

Its value is demonstrating the **causal substrate**, not the higher-order theory.

## 3. Slice 2 — epistemic routes, recognition, and plural social worlds

Slice 2 introduces the distinction between objective state and actor-specific knowledge/recognition.

The deeper question becomes:

> Can socially meaningful beliefs become causal without being promoted into objective truth or globally synchronized engine knowledge?

The accepted v0 model already separates:

- objective facts;
- provenance-bearing `KnownFact`;
- `Recognition` as a social interpretation;
- unknown / recognized / contested states;
- stale information;
- direct participation/observation;
- communicated evidence.

This is a seed for later questions involving legitimacy, disputed authority, institutional memory, foreign recognition, and competing genealogies.

### The important epistemic route

Future Slice-2 design/implementation should preserve the ability to reconstruct routes such as:

`event → participation / observation / report → KnownFact → Recognition → changed action availability`

The exact mechanism remains governed by accepted Slice-2 semantics. The point of preserving the route conceptually is to avoid recognition becoming an unexplained boolean that merely mirrors engine truth.

### Signals worth observing

After Slice 2 is runnable, inspect:

- whether two actors can possess materially different knowledge of the same objective world;
- whether stale or contested information persists visibly;
- whether recognition changes action possibilities;
- whether recognition can lag objective change;
- whether an actor can know an event yet interpret its social meaning differently;
- whether causal provenance of knowledge remains inspectable.

### Seeds of later institutional questions

If the representation works, it supplies ingredients for:

- internal versus external recognition;
- legitimate versus merely effective authority;
- remembered versus objective history;
- later documentary transmission;
- outsider reconstruction;
- succession disputes.

These remain future questions, not Slice-2 requirements.

## 4. Slice 3 — emergence, reification, continuity, and grounded capability

Slice 3 is the first planned point where lower-level social reality produces a persistent higher-order entity.

Before formation there are people, residences, ties, support history, recognition, and candidate evidence.

A `CandidateOrganization` may represent a possible organization, but it has no household authority or resources merely because the engine can label it.

A successful historical process can then produce a persistent Household with a `FormationWarrant`.

The deeper question is:

> Can the simulation reify a higher-order entity from lower-level history without making the resulting identity primitive or magical?

### Identity versus justification

A stable Household ID is useful engine bookkeeping.

The historically interesting issue is what makes continued use of that identifier semantically defensible.

Candidate ingredients in v0 include:

- formation provenance;
- sustaining participants;
- participation warrants;
- explicit entry/exit;
- continuity bearers;
- lifecycle transitions;
- predecessor/lineage information.

This directly exercises the distinction:

**numerical identity ≠ historical continuity ≠ current composition.**

### Operational state versus identity

The accepted Active / Inactive / Dissolved states are explicitly operational.

This gives the project an early test of:

**identity ≠ operational state.**

A household can remain historically the same household while temporarily lacking full operational capability.

### Grounded capability

`HouseholdProvisionCommitment(Person → Household)` is especially important conceptually.

It offers a route for a collective to possess real causal capability without acquiring unexplained pooled resources simply because a Household object exists.

This begins testing:

**identity ≠ capability.**

### Triggered mine: candidate convergence

The already-deferred `LATER-01` question about same-candidate equivalence/convergence should be revisited only when household formation implementation makes multiple candidate labels/evidence paths executable.

That problem is a pre-identity version of the larger continuity question:

> When do multiple descriptions/evidence paths refer to one emerging organization rather than several?

Do not generalize the answer beyond the bounded household problem unless later evidence warrants it.

### Signals worth observing

After Slice 3 is runnable, inspect:

- whether a household's formation can be reconstructed from lower-level history;
- whether deleting the Household label from a human explanation would still leave an intelligible causal story of how it formed;
- whether constituent turnover preserves identity only through explicit historical bridges;
- whether capability can disappear while identity persists;
- whether household-originated action remains grounded in real participants/resources;
- whether the implementation starts relying on snapshot similarity despite the accepted model.

## 5. Slice 4 — office continuity and action through representation

Slice 4 introduces the first persistent representative/head role and occupant turnover.

The deeper question becomes:

> Can a higher-order actor act through a person while preserving the distinction between the collective, the office, the office-holder, authority, recognition, and capability?

This is the first direct experiment with an **office** in the broader sense suggested by the Uruk corpus.

### Distinctions to preserve

Potentially separable facts include:

- Household H exists;
- HeadRole R exists;
- Person P currently occupies R;
- P has authority in a particular scope;
- Actor A recognizes P as current holder;
- Actor B possesses stale evidence and recognizes the predecessor;
- H currently has enough backing/capacity to perform an action.

These should not silently collapse into one `Head=true` fact.

### Seeds of later succession problems

Even a simple v0 succession can expose machinery relevant to:

- office persistence despite occupant change;
- vacancy without identity loss;
- stale recognition after succession;
- former-holder influence versus present authority;
- eventual competing claimants or usurpation;
- action in personal mode versus representative mode.

The richer cases remain outside v0 unless already accepted.

### Signals worth observing

After Slice 4 is runnable, inspect:

- whether household action can be causally reconstructed through the role-holder;
- whether personal and representative agency remain distinguishable;
- whether vacancy preserves household identity but removes relevant capability;
- whether succession changes occupant identity without changing office identity;
- whether recognition can lag succession without rewriting objective occupancy.

## 6. Slice 5 — causal sufficiency through continuation

Slice 5 adds delayed processes and checkpoint/restore continuation.

Although partly an engineering problem, it creates a powerful conceptual audit:

> What state must survive interruption for the future to unfold identically?

Anything whose omission changes future behavior is causally relevant, regardless of whether designers considered it important.

Checkpointing can therefore function as a practical **causal-sufficiency audit** of the reference model.

Future-influencing state may include:

- people and material resources;
- relationships/obligations;
- subjective knowledge and recognition;
- household identity/lifecycle;
- role occupancy;
- pending processes;
- cooldown/reconsideration baselines;
- identifier/order state;
- rule/configuration identity.

### Delayed processes as extended causality

A delayed process also lets one action remain causally active across multiple future cycles.

That form may later support richer phenomena such as journeys, work, construction, legal/administrative processes, obligations, or institutional routines.

Those are future possibilities, not v0 commitments.

### Signals worth observing

After Slice 5 is runnable, inspect:

- whether checkpoint omission tests reveal unexpected future-influencing state;
- whether uninterrupted and restored histories remain causally equivalent;
- whether delayed work retains explicit prerequisites and provenance;
- whether transient runtime machinery accidentally became authoritative state;
- whether all persistent institutional/subjective facts needed for future action actually survive continuation.

## 7. Genealogy may accumulate rather than require one future subsystem

The Institutional Dynamics framework distinguishes several genealogies.

SFL v0 suggests these might eventually arise through composition rather than one universal genealogy engine.

Possible ingredients:

- Slice 1: objective causal event history;
- Slice 2: actor-held remembered/reported histories;
- Slice 3: formation, participation, continuity, and lineage provenance;
- Slice 4: office/succession history;
- future artifact systems: official/documentary genealogy.

This is only a hypothesis.

The useful design lesson is to avoid prematurely creating one genealogy abstraction merely because several future phenomena involve ancestry or history.

## 8. Questions v0 does not answer

Even a successful five-slice v0 leaves major Uruk-derived areas substantially open:

- tasks and responsibility networks;
- skill/knowledge acquisition through work;
- delegation/supervision/coordination cost;
- documentary artifact identity/copying/authenticity;
- informal roles and effective centrality;
- institutional culture;
- multiple simultaneous formal authority claimants;
- richer motivation, attention, and bodily capacity;
- genuine archival destruction/reconstruction from imperfect memory;
- generalized institutional identity beyond households.

Do not pull these systems into v0 merely because the current slices expose conceptual prerequisites.

## 9. Recommended future stance

As each slice becomes timely, ask:

> **Which larger questions does this slice naturally make observable, without expanding what the slice is required to implement?**

This preserves the Uruk corpus as a guiding light rather than a specification.

The preferred sequence is:

1. implement the bounded accepted slice;
2. verify it;
3. observe what the new dimension actually does;
4. retrieve the relevant conceptual questions;
5. classify any interesting finding;
6. promote a new mechanism only if a concrete later need and adequate evidence justify it.
