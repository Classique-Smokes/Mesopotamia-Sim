# Retired Vizier Extensions to Institutional Dynamics

**Status:** DURABLE NON-AUTHORITATIVE / PROVISIONAL EXTENSION  
**Source basis:** `../THE_RETIRED_VIZIER_TABLETS_FIRST_COLLECTION/`  
**Authority:** None.

The Retired Vizier corpus mostly deepens existing Institutional Dynamics themes rather than creating a separate umbrella.

## 1. Role-conditioned salience / institutional internalization

The corpus suggests that sustained office-holding can shape what a person notices, prioritizes, and experiences as unfinished work.

Important distinction:

> role-conditioned cognition may create both expertise and blindness.

This is not yet a mechanic and should not be reduced to an office bonus/penalty scalar.

Future questions include:

- which role pressures alter salience;
- whether those pressures persist after office;
- how quickly they decay;
- how expertise survives while role obligation ends;
- whether deliberate distance/rotation changes perception.

This is outside current SFL v0 agency scope.

## 2. Post-office social succession

Formal occupancy can change while:

- friendship persists;
- remembered legitimacy persists;
- practical influence persists;
- old subordinates retain habits;
- access to the former holder remains valuable.

This extends the existing Returning Predecessor challenge.

Formal succession and social succession are separable.

A successful transition may require deliberate predecessor non-action as well as successor action.

## 3. Residual legitimacy versus decaying competence

A former holder may retain prestige and access after current operational knowledge starts to decay.

This should remain separate from:

- formal authority;
- current competence;
- effective influence;
- recognition by others.

## 4. Structural failure under sincere actors

The Obedient Scribes case reinforces that institutional failure can arise from:

- limited perspective;
- common-mode reasoning;
- ambiguous instructions;
- decomposition;
- hidden coupling;
- evidence scope mismatch;
- integration scarcity.

No corruption is required.

Human motives may worsen or repair these failures; they should not be assumed to have one sign.

## 5. Institutional corrigibility

The Archive and Priest tablets together add a stronger theme:

> institutions need socially and procedurally viable paths by which evidence can contradict authoritative claims.

Sub-distinctions:

- authority to act is not authority over truth;
- good-faith institutional records can be false;
- correction should preserve the history of error when that error was causally real;
- verification is itself institutional labor;
- criticism requires social conditions in which contradiction is survivable;
- credible opposition can increase trust;
- adversarial roles may need strong negative authority without broad positive sovereignty.

## 6. Existing mechanism families affected

These observations primarily extend:

- Offices / roles separate from holders;
- Authority claims;
- Recognition and legitimacy;
- Artifacts and documentary memory;
- Procedures / practices;
- Tasks, responsibility, skill and knowledge;
- Effective centrality / influence.

They do **not** justify a universal institution ontology.

## 7. Current-slice boundary

These extensions are mostly later than Slice 2 or outside v0 entirely.

The archive/correction and sanctioned-opposition themes may eventually interact with documentary memory, office succession, and institutional epistemics.

Role-conditioned salience belongs to richer agency/attention work, not the current minimal subjective-information slice.


## 8. Redundancy / overlap disposition

The corpus should not create duplicate mechanism families for distinctions already represented elsewhere.

### Already established — no new family

- office/role separate from holder;
- formal authority distinct from effective influence;
- recognition distinct from objective truth;
- official/remembered history distinct from causal history;
- durable artifact not automatically true;
- institutional action grounded in people/roles rather than a collective ghost.

The tablets remain useful challenge cases for these distinctions.

### Genuine extensions

- role-conditioned salience / expertise-and-blindness;
- post-office **social** succession and predecessor shadow;
- residual legitimacy versus decaying competence;
- institutional error under sincere actors as a coordination/epistemic problem;
- correction history and institutional corrigibility;
- sanctioned opposition / credible contradiction.

### Primary cross-links

- post-office succession → mechanism families B/C/D/K and `02_URUK_CHALLENGE_CASES.md` “Returning Predecessor”;
- structural failure under sincere actors → mechanism family J;
- archive correction → mechanism families H/L;
- sanctioned opposition → mechanism families C/D/I;
- role-conditioned salience → future richer agency/attention seam, with J as the closest existing institutional family.
