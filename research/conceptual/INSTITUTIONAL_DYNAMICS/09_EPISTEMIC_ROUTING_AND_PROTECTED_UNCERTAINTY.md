## Why this seam exists

The conceptual library already distinguishes actor-specific knowledge, formal authority, recognition, artifacts, procedures, and objective causal history.

The first-contact and supporting-context corpora add a narrower question that is not captured by any one of those distinctions:

> Can an institution persist partly through learned conventions about **what kind of claim can be settled by what kind of evidence, artifact, authority, office, or procedure**?

This is not a new top-level program. It is a future seam inside Institutional Dynamics, especially families G (recognition/legitimacy), H (artifacts/documentary memory), and I (procedures/practices), with a secondary Social Epistemic Dynamics lens.

## 1. Institutional epistemic routing

Actor-relative knowledge answers questions such as who has heard, observed, inferred, or recognized a claim.

Institutional epistemic routing asks a different question:

> Which socially recognized route is treated as competent to settle this kind of claim?

Candidate distinctions include:

- direct observation versus documentary proof;
- technical demonstration versus lived acceptance;
- office authority versus evidentiary competence;
- archival record versus causal genealogy;
- procedural validation versus substantive truth;
- local practice versus formal recognition;
- one artifact genre versus another artifact genre.

The same evidence may therefore have different institutional force depending on the type of question being asked.

This seam must not become a universal “truth regime” scalar. Any future design should preserve the underlying actors, artifacts, procedures, authority relations, and causal evidence.

## 2. Protected uncertainty / recognized non-decision

The supporting corpus also preserves a distinct possibility:

> “Open,” disputed, outside current competence, or not-yet-earned can itself be a durable institutional state.

This is more than ignorance.

Potential future questions include:

- who may mark a matter unresolved;
- whether different offices recognize different competence boundaries;
- what evidence or procedure is sufficient to close an open matter;
- whether uncertainty survives succession;
- whether reopening a settled issue differs from resolving a question that was never settled;
- whether pressure for premature closure creates systematic error.

No generic `OpenQuestion` object is authorized by this note.

## 3. Question transformation by epistemic culture

The first-contact experiment suggests a composite challenge:

> The same question can become a different problem before it is answered.

An imported founder, continuity, identity, or persistence question may be reframed by a receiving institution into evidence status, risk, reconstructability, succession, or authority-boundary questions because those are the categories its inherited practices make salient.

This composes:

- Social Epistemic Dynamics — route, representation, and transformation;
- role-conditioned salience — what becomes noticeable;
- Institutional Dynamics — authority, procedures, artifacts, and genealogy;
- cross-boundary transmission — selective adoption and semantic transformation.

The future falsification case should therefore compare identical or closely matched inputs across different institutional epistemic cultures and ask whether problem-framing diverges before answer content diverges.

## 4. Relationship to first-contact transmission

First-contact Wave A adds two closely related extensions.

### Semantic-fidelity transmission

A derivative vocabulary can preserve operational relations—such as who may decide, what evidence supports which claim, and what remains outside scope—while changing metaphor, salience, and memory.

Primary home: Institutional Dynamics H, with Social Epistemic Dynamics as a secondary lens.

### Reflexive observation / endogenous archival feedback

An observer, question, audit, translation, or critique may change the institution being observed. The resulting artifact or practice can then become part of the institution's state and later become evidence used to reconstruct the intervention itself.

Primary home: Institutional Dynamics H/I/L, cross-linked to Social Epistemic Dynamics.

This is distinct from a merely partial foreign observer. The observer is part of the causal chain.

## 5. Anti-shortcuts

Do not infer that:

- an authoritative office determines truth merely because it can decide;
- an archive is true merely because it is official;
- a question has one culturally neutral formulation;
- non-decision is absence of institutional state;
- one evidence type can impersonate another;
- a translated concept is faithful merely because the words look similar;
- a changed vocabulary proves the operational proposition changed;
- an observer can always be modeled as causally external to the observed institution.

## 7. Promotion boundary

This file preserves questions, not answers.

Any future promotion requires independent evidence/design work that establishes:

- the modeled domain in which institutional claim-routing conventions matter;
- the primitives needed to represent them without duplicating authority, knowledge, artifact, and procedure machinery;
- falsification cases separating epistemic routing from ordinary disagreement or information scarcity;
- verification that protected uncertainty is socially represented rather than merely an engine enum;
- evidence that question transformation has causal consequences worth modeling.

Until then, this is a retrieval and research seam only.
