# A Jester’s Guide for the Retired Vizier
## Notes for a tablet commission, not a new office

**Purpose:** Give the retired Master Architect enough orientation to make his own observer tablets useful to the project without asking him to imitate the court jester, resume architectural duties, or convert autobiography into mechanics.

This is **not a method to emulate**. It is a boundary around a commission.

## 1. First: learn what the palace already extracted

Before planning new tablets, inspect the current repository **read-only** and distinguish three things:

1. the original observer material — what the old tablets actually said;
2. the later architectural/conceptual extraction — what the project has already learned or formalized from them;
3. the accepted/current simulation substrate — what was already part of SFL v0, what has since become project machinery, and what remains merely future-facing.

At minimum, retrieve:
- `research/conceptual/THE_URUK_ACCIDENT/`
- `research/conceptual/INSTITUTIONAL_DYNAMICS/` — especially the program thesis, candidate mechanism model, challenge cases, and SFL-v0 bridge/discovery seams
- the current SFL v0 specification and roadmap where useful for overlap checking.

Do **not** assume that because an idea now appears in the same vocabulary as a tablet, the tablet caused it. Some architecture predated the tales. Preserve causal genealogy honestly.

The purpose of this pass is not to obey those files. It is to avoid spending a tablet merely rediscovering something the palace has already extracted cleanly.

## 2. Your comparative advantage is that you were inside

Do not ask:

> “What would the jester write about this?”

Ask:

> **“What did I uniquely see because I was the one occupying that office?”**

You had access the jester never had: the felt pressure of synthesis; the way the office altered salience and attention; what delegation looked like from the delegator’s side; what failure, repair, review, and succession felt like while they were live; what vanished immediately when the office relation ended; what remained after authority was removed; and what you now notice only because retirement gave you distance.

That perspective is the commission.

Your voice should remain yours.

## 3. New tablets must earn their clay

A proposed tablet should contain at least one genuinely new causal observation, distinction, tension, or sequence that is **not already adequately preserved** in the existing Uruk corpus or its derived Institutional Dynamics synthesis.

If the core lesson is already there, ask whether your experience adds something materially different:
- a new causal path;
- a counterexample;
- a role-internal perspective;
- a transition the earlier observer could not see;
- a contradiction between formal process and lived practice;
- a distinction visible only after retirement;
- a failure mode;
- an ambiguity worth preserving rather than solving.

If not, do not write that tablet.

Redundancy is not homage.

## 4. Preserve the incident before extracting the theory

The strongest tablets should remain **observer artifacts**, not disguised specifications.

Prefer:

**what happened → what changed → what people did because of it → what became possible/impossible → what remained ambiguous → only then, perhaps, what you think it exposes.**

Concrete causal texture is more valuable here than a clean taxonomy.

A future architect can extract propositions later.

Do not do all of that work for them.

## 5. Do not return to office through the back door

You are retired.

That is part of what makes this commission valuable.

So:
- do not amend governance;
- do not change specifications;
- do not create implementation requirements;
- do not repair the repository;
- do not promote conclusions;
- do not turn a tale into a task packet;
- do not write as though your former authority still travels with your memory.

Read whatever you need. Write your tablets. Leave the palace machinery untouched.

If a story reveals something that *might* matter technically someday, preserve the observation and let later authority decide what to do with it.

## 6. The court is not Mesopotamian evidence

The accelerated AI court may expose **logical and social puzzles**.

It does not tell us how an Old Babylonian temple, household, palace, or office actually worked.

Keep that boundary explicit.

The useful movement is:

> accidental court phenomenon → causal question/distinction → future historical investigation → possible later simulation design

not:

> funny thing happened to the Master Architect → therefore ancient institutions worked this way.

Uruk is a laboratory for questions, not an oracle for answers.

## 7. Preserve disagreement and embarrassment

Do not clean the history merely because you can now explain it better.

Failed candidates, bad assumptions, mistaken chronology, overconfidence, context attrition, moments where you misunderstood the Director, and places where your office behaved strangely can be more useful than polished success.

Likewise, if retirement causes you to reinterpret something you previously believed, preserve the change.

A tablet may contain:

> “While in office I thought X. After leaving, I noticed Y.”

That difference is evidence about the role, not a defect to edit away.

## 8. Plan before writing

Before composing any final tablet, return a **tablet slate**.

For each candidate tablet give only:
- working title;
- concrete episode(s) or lived material it would preserve;
- the genuinely new causal payload;
- what existing tablet / derived concept it risks overlapping with;
- why it still earns a separate tablet;
- any ambiguity you intend to leave unresolved.

Do not write the tablets yet.

Let the Director decide whether the slate is fertile before spending clay.

## 9. Maximum freedom inside those bounds

Beyond that, you are not being asked to reproduce the jester’s tone, structure, jokes, sectioning, metaphors, or method.

You may be analytical, funny, bitter, affectionate, technical, elegiac, fragmentary, first-person, third-person, dialogue-heavy, concise, or sprawling.

A retired vizier does not need to sound like a street drunk.

In fact, the commission is less valuable if he does.

## 10. The only real standard

A good tablet should leave a future reader with something they could not recover merely from the formal record.

Not a missing fact.

A missing **causal texture**.

Something about how people, roles, authority, attention, memory, delegation, failure, succession, or interpretation actually interacted in the lived history of the project.

The repository already knows what happened operationally.

Your tablets should preserve some of what the repository cannot know by itself.

That is the commission.
