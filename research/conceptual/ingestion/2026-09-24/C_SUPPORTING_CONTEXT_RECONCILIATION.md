## 1. Bottom line

This collection is deliberately mixed.

It contains:

- real conceptual payload;
- later synthesis of ideas already extracted elsewhere;
- observer/commission methodology;
- first-contact transfer and provenance context;
- project/governance analogy;
- explanatory narrative written for a human recipient.

The collection therefore should **not** be promoted as one new concept program.

The strongest genuinely reusable delta not already stated cleanly in the current global conceptual structure is:

1. **Institutional epistemic routing / epistemic constitution** — institutions may persist partly through conventions about *what kind of claim can be settled by what kind of evidence, authority, artifact, or procedure*. This is more than actor-specific information provenance and more than ordinary office authority. It is a candidate **new future seam inside Institutional Dynamics**, cross-linked to Social Epistemic Dynamics, not a new top-level umbrella.
2. **Protected uncertainty / recognized non-decision** — an institution may durably preserve that a question is *open, disputed, outside current competence, or not yet earned*, rather than forcing a settled answer. This is a candidate **new future seam** spanning procedures, authority, artifacts, and institutional epistemics.
3. **Question transformation by institutional epistemic culture** — two institutions can receive the same hard question or event yet convert it into different follow-up problems because their inherited roles, evidence practices, and salience structures route attention differently. This is best preserved as a **cross-program challenge**, not as a standalone mechanism family.

Several other ideas are genuine and reusable, but they are best treated as **extensions of existing families**: genre-conditioned self-representation, symbolic versus practice continuity, historical/self-descriptive reflexivity, artifact-purpose selection of ancestry, dimension-specific recoverability, cultural translation and recognition, and the causal effect of interpretations.

Much of the rest is already durably absorbed: office/holder separation, authority versus influence/capability/recognition, official versus causal genealogy, anti-collective-ghost grounding, role-conditioned salience, archival rupture, and engine truth versus in-world evidence.

The commission-writing rules themselves are mostly **observer methodology**, not simulation concepts.

## 5. The genuinely new delta

### 5.1 Institutional epistemic routing / “epistemic constitution”

The anthology’s strongest unabsorbed generalization appears on pp. 34–35 and is reinforced by the Uruk/Conservatory epistemic-style comparison on p. 46 and the future “epistemic institutions” prompts on pp. 50–51.

The important claim is not merely:

> actors know different things.

The project already has actor-relative knowledge and provenance.

Nor is it merely:

> offices have authority.

Institutional Dynamics already separates authority from recognition, capability, influence, and truth.

The new question is:

> **Can an institution persist partly through learned conventions for routing claims to recognized kinds of evidence, authority, artifact, or procedure?**

Examples of the abstract distinction include:

- one kind of artifact can establish debt but not ritual validity;
- an office can settle a procedural question but not a lineage fact;
- direct observation can answer one claim while formal recognition is still required for another;
- two institutions can see materially similar evidence but route it through different validity procedures.

This is a real causal question beyond “how the project writes documents.”

Recommended disposition:

**NEW FUTURE CONCEPT FAMILY / SEAM — inside Institutional Dynamics, not a new umbrella program.**

Closest existing homes:

- G — Recognition and legitimacy;
- H — Artifacts and documentary memory;
- I — Procedures / practices;
- Social Epistemic Dynamics — information routes and actor-held representations.

The seam is specifically about **institution-level claim-routing conventions** and who/what is recognized as competent to settle a claim.

### 5.2 Protected uncertainty / recognized non-decision

The anthology generalizes Wonder Gather’s practical `Open` status into a wider institutional possibility:

> **an institution can preserve a question as deliberately unresolved, contested, outside present competence, or awaiting stronger evidence.**

This differs from simple ignorance.

The state is socially and institutionally recognized.

Potential later questions include:

- who can mark a matter open;
- what evidence is sufficient to settle it;
- whether different offices recognize different boundaries of competence;
- whether an unresolved question survives succession;
- whether reopening a settled issue is procedurally different from resolving an open one.

Recommended disposition:

**NEW FUTURE CONCEPT FAMILY / SEAM — subordinate to institutional epistemics/procedures.**

Do not turn this into a generic `OpenQuestion` object without domain evidence.

### 5.3 Question transformation by institutional epistemic culture

The hard-question experiment produced a distinct dynamic:

- Uruk asked founder, continuity, voice, and persistence questions;
- the receiving project responded through truth-surface mapping, evidence status, risk, succession, and reconstructability;
- the same input therefore became a different problem before being answered.

The reusable causal question is:

> **Can inherited concepts, roles, procedures, and evidence practices change which problem an actor or institution believes it is facing?**

This is not merely “different answers.”

It is **problem transformation**.

Recommended disposition:

**NEW REUSABLE DISTINCTION + CROSS-PROGRAM CHALLENGE.**

It naturally composes:

- Social Epistemic Dynamics — route and representation;
- role-conditioned salience — what becomes noticeable;
- institutional epistemics — what counts as a legitimate way to resolve a claim;
- cultural transmission — what changes when a question crosses a social/institutional border.

Task D should consider a composite challenge rather than a new top-level program.

## 6. Genuine extensions that should not become new families

### Genre-conditioned self-representation

The same institution can produce diplomatic, historical, and operational descriptions that select different causal resolutions.

This is stronger than “official history may be incomplete,” but it still belongs under:

- artifacts/documentary memory;
- multiple genealogies;
- self-description/reflexivity.

The useful future question is what artifact purpose makes salient or suppresses, not whether an institution has one hidden “true voice.”

### Symbolic continuity versus practice continuity

A named cultural layer can disappear while workflows and authority relations persist.

Conversely, names and historical language can survive after practice changes.

This is a useful decomposition of continuity, not a new ontology.

### Institutional reflexivity / self-description becoming causal

Histories and role descriptions may become part of the future causal environment.

That mechanism is a direct extension of the existing externalized-causality thesis:

**artifact / description → later interpretation → action → changed institution.**

No “myth meter” or universal reflexivity mechanic follows.

### Dimension-specific recoverability

Repositories, archives, or successor procedures may preserve state while losing semantic nuance, taste, motive, alternatives, or authority continuity.

This sharpens artifact/memory and succession questions but does not require a separate family.

### Emotional salience and memory survival

The project case suggests that funny, humiliating, tragic, prestigious, or affectionate episodes are disproportionately preserved.

That is worth a cross-program challenge, but the current evidence is project anecdote. It should not yet become a generalized memory model.

### Norm knowledge versus compliance

An actor or institution can sincerely remember a warning and still repeat the behavior under situated pressure.

This is a useful challenge to rules-as-possession shortcuts, but not enough here for a standalone family.

## 7. Observer / commission methodology — preserve, do not mechanize

The two guide documents contain strong method discipline.

These instructions are valuable to the conceptual archive because they help produce better observer sources:

- compare against existing extracted theory before commissioning more stories;
- preserve causal genealogy honestly;
- exploit role-specific vantage rather than imitate another observer’s style;
- require a genuinely new causal observation before creating another tablet;
- preserve incidents before abstracting them;
- keep source artifacts separate from later extraction;
- do not let a retired observer re-enter authority through narrative;
- keep project analogies distinct from historical evidence;
- preserve failed assumptions, embarrassment, and reinterpretation;
- separate direct event, institutional self-description, and observer inference;
- use controlled/blind delivery where answer priming matters;
- freeze responses before later discussion changes the evidence;
- preserve non-answers rather than repairing them into elegant theory.

These are **observer/commission methodology**, not simulation mechanisms.

The generalized lesson is epistemic humility and provenance discipline, not “how Mesopotamian actors write stories.”

## 8. Project/governance analogy and AI-specific material

Several recurring observations are real in the project but should not be promoted as AI-specific social ontology:

### Repository as durable memory

In this project, Git/repository artifacts preserve retrievable state across agent turnover.

Reusable conceptual kernel:

- durable external memory and live interpretive/semantic continuity are separable;
- succession can preserve one while degrading the other.

Disposition:

**existing artifact/memory + succession family.**

Do **not** create an “AI institutional memory” family.

### High-context integrator versus archive

The Threadkeeper / Chieftain material distinguishes:

- carrying retrievable state;
- integrating meaning across state.

Reusable conceptual kernel:

- storage and integration are different work;
- concentration of both can create a coordination/bottleneck risk.

Disposition:

**existing Work/Knowledge/Coordination + Artifacts/Memory**, with project-governance analogy as source context.

### Agent mortality / office survival

The project’s succession experience is vivid, but the base office/holder distinction is already absorbed.

Disposition:

**redundant core; source retained.**

### Independent review / negative authority

This collection mentions reviewers and bounded contradiction, but the Retired Vizier reconciliation already extracts sanctioned opposition / credible contradiction.

Disposition:

**existing Retired Vizier extension / governance analogy.**

### Project as accelerated social laboratory

The project can reveal puzzles because roles, artifacts, succession, translation, and memory transitions happen quickly and are unusually well recorded.

It cannot establish general social laws by introspection.

Disposition:

**observer methodology only.**

## 13. Final disposition

The supporting-context collection contains real theory, but its value is **not** proportional to its volume.

The durable conceptual delta is small and useful:

- institutions may inherit **ways of deciding what counts as a valid answer**, not only beliefs and rules;
- institutions may preserve **recognized uncertainty** rather than force closure;
- inherited epistemic culture may **transform the problem itself** before actors answer it.

Around that core, the corpus provides strong extensions concerning self-description, historical causality, symbolic/practice continuity, artifact genre, and recoverability.

Everything else should remain where it belongs: existing concept families, first-contact Wave A, observer methodology, provenance, explanation, or project/governance analogy.
