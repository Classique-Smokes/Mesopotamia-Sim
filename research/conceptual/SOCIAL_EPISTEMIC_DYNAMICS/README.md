# Social Epistemic Dynamics — Provisional Conceptual Program

**Status:** DURABLE NON-AUTHORITATIVE / PROVISIONAL GUIDING FRAMEWORK  
**Source basis:** `../THE_ROADS_BETWEEN_PEOPLE_THIRD_JESTER_COMMISSION/`  
**Authority:** None. This does not modify SFL v0, architecture, verification, or roadmap scope.

## Why this exists

The Roads corpus does not fit cleanly inside Institutional Dynamics because its central mechanisms can exist before any higher-order institution exists.

Its organizing question is:

> How do social relationships become routes through which actors construct representations of other actors and events, and how do those representations recursively reshape the relationships and networks that carried them?

The core loop is:

**social relation → information route → selection/transformation → actor-held representation → action → changed relation/network → changed future information routes**

## Distinctions to preserve

- social topology is not identical to epistemic topology;
- transmitted content is not independent of route;
- direct observation does not erase mediated prehistory automatically;
- coordinated group behavior does not imply one group mind;
- reputation should not default to one global scalar;
- brokerage power is relational/structural, not an intrinsic trait;
- plural social realities can be causally effective without requiring equal evidentiary support;
- false or compressed representations may have real consequences.

## Candidate future question families

### Mediated relationship ancestry

A direct relationship may have causal prehistory involving third parties who are no longer participants in the dyad.

### Group information environments without group minds

Dense groups may coordinate from asymmetric individual knowledge without possessing one shared belief state.

### Distributed social representation

A person's socially effective image may exist as many actor-held representations rather than one authoritative reputation value.

### Brokerage and informational centrality

Influence may emerge from being difficult to bypass in communication/introduction/trust routes, before any office exists.

### Plural network memory

Clustered transmission, trust, compression and later path dependence may produce locally coherent but conflicting social histories.

## Relationship to SFL v0

Slice 2 already contains a deliberately narrower accepted epistemic substrate:

- actor-relative KnownFact;
- Recognition distinct from objective truth;
- provenance-bearing communication;
- stale information;
- contested recognition;
- no global synchronization.

That substrate is compatible with some Roads questions but intentionally excludes much of the corpus:

- generic trust;
- deliberate lying;
- rumor mutation;
- reputation systems;
- generalized brokerage power;
- rich memory degradation;
- generic communication-network simulation.

The Roads corpus therefore supplies **future observational/challenge questions**, not missing Slice-2 requirements.

## Relationship to Institutional Dynamics

Institutional Dynamics concerns persistent higher-order actors, authority, capability, memory, genealogy, offices and recognition.

Social Epistemic Dynamics sits partly below that level. Institutional phenomena may later use these mechanisms, but they are not institution-specific.

Keep the programs separate and connect them only through explicit later research/design work.


## Systematized horizon

The source corpus contains both current-slice lenses and much later ideas.

### Useful during Slice 2 now — no new semantics

Use the Roads corpus to challenge whether Slice 2 preserves:

- real actor-to-actor divergence in subjective state;
- provenance/route identity on communicated information;
- stale information rather than passive synchronization;
- contested social interpretation without arbitrary winner selection;
- anti-omniscience and no magical household/group mind;
- visible causal effects from subjective state.

These are **interpretive/verification lenses only**. Accepted Slice-2 semantics remain controlling.

### Future ideas preserved here

- mediated relationship ancestry;
- group information environments without group minds;
- distributed social representation/reputation;
- plural network memory;
- social communication routes as network structure.

### Shared family: brokerage / informational centrality

Brokerage belongs primarily to **Institutional Dynamics K — Effective centrality / influence**, because that family already covers brokerage, unique knowledge, communication flows and informal roles.

The Roads corpus adds the social-epistemic side:

- bypass cost;
- translation/interpretation;
- repeated route dependence;
- broker burden/discretion;
- hidden dependency revealed by broker removal.

Treat these as one shared family viewed at different levels, not parallel theories.
