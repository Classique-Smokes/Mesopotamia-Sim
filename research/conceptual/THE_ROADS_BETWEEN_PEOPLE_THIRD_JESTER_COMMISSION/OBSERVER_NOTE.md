# Observer Note — Questions, Extrapolations, and Deliberate Non-Decisions
## Companion note to *The Roads Between People*

**Status:** Non-authoritative observer note

### What the collection directly tries to preserve

The tablets preserve five causal questions:

1. Can a third party shape a relationship before the principals meet?
2. Can a small dense group coordinate around asymmetrically distributed knowledge without possessing one group mind?
3. Can reputation exist as a distributed family of representations rather than one scalar property?
4. Can network brokerage create power, burden, and dependency before formal office appears?
5. Can clustered communication histories generate durable plural social realities without centralized deception?

### What is extrapolation

The Mesopotamian skin is illustrative.

These tales are **not historical evidence** that ancient Mesopotamian households, merchants, quarters, or institutions behaved in these exact ways.

The broader claims are conceptual prompts:

- social ties can be information routes;
- source route may affect trust;
- living transmission can transform information;
- information and relationships can recursively alter one another;
- higher-order patterns may arise from many subjective states without a collective ghost.

Whether these mechanisms mattered in a particular period, city, archive, household form, or institution requires historical research.

### What remains deliberately unresolved

This collection does not decide:

- what counts as a primitive communication event;
- how trust should be represented;
- whether reputation deserves first-class state;
- whether household-level orientations should ever be explicitly represented;
- how memory degradation should work;
- how deliberate deception differs mechanically from sincere distortion;
- how communication cost scales;
- whether brokerage should be derived from network structure or represented through other mechanisms;
- how written artifacts later interact with living transmission;
- how institutional offices alter or formalize these social routes.

Those are design and evidence questions for later work.

### The hinge

The collection's narrowest common claim is:

> **People are not only related to other people. They are also routes through which other people become socially real.**

That relation between **social topology** and **epistemic topology** may be worth watching as the simulation acquires subjective knowledge, households, roles, and later institutions.

No mechanic follows automatically from the observation.
