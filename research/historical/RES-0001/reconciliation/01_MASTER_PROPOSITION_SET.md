# RES-0001R — Master Proposition Set

**Authority:** Advisory reconciliation only. These `HEC-` identifiers are reconciliation candidate IDs, not canonical Historical Evidence Register (`HE-`) entries.

**Scope:** Old Babylonian Nippur household-foundations evidence supplied in RES-0001 returns A–H. Comparative material is not promoted as local evidence.

**Method:** Claims were deduplicated at the level of the underlying ancient document, archaeological context, dossier, and modern reconstruction. Agreement among research agents is not counted as independent corroboration.

**Design status for every proposition:** `NO DESIGN DECISION`.

## Reconciled propositions

### HEC-0001 — Measured house property is not a household roster

- **Claim / proposition:** Measured or transferred é-property in the inspected Nippur records describes premises, dimensions, fixtures, or property interests; it does not by itself enumerate a complete residential group.
- **Period / date range:** Rīm-Sîn 48 through Ilī-ma-ilum 1 in the directly cited examples.
- **Geography / archive / context:** Nippur, TA-associated house-property sale/exchange records; archaeological phase labels retained as catalogued.
- **Underlying primary evidence:** SAOC 44, 17 (P283587), obv. 1–13; SAOC 44, 12 (P257360), obv. 1–15.
- **Relevant secondary scholarship:** Stone 1987 (SAOC 44) supplies the excavation/text catalogue context; no broader lexical theory is required for the narrow distinction.
- **RES-0001 source returns:** H; supported by the property/residence cautions in B and F.
- **Evidence character:** DIRECT EVIDENCE + SOURCE-CRITICAL SYNTHESIS
- **Disagreements / variant readings:** Some uses of é can be broader than a measured property. No exclusive lexical definition of é is asserted.
- **Confidence:** **HIGH**
- **Confidence rationale:** The cited instruments directly describe measured property rather than a roster of residents; the negative limit follows from what the texts actually enumerate.
- **Limits on generalization:** These records do not establish that nobody lived in the properties; they establish only that the property descriptions are not complete occupancy lists.
- **Relevance to simulation questions (descriptive only):** Separates premises/property evidence from evidence for persons and residence.
- **Design status:** NO DESIGN DECISION

### HEC-0002 — Residence is a distinct, explicitly attestable relation

- **Claim / proposition:** Old Babylonian Nippur records can state residence explicitly, while contribution to a house acquisition, property ownership, or temporary tenancy/access remains a different kind of association.
- **Period / date range:** Samsu-iluna 7–8 in the directly compared TA examples.
- **Geography / archive / context:** Nippur TA records; house-payment account and property/tenancy records.
- **Underlying primary evidence:** SAOC 44, 25 (P224340), obv. 11–14, with ša ina é ... wašbu; B-return TA contributor/tenant examples D11/D19 as catalogued by Stone 1987.
- **Relevant secondary scholarship:** Stone 1987, especially the TA catalogue and Asirum index discussion; Stone’s occupancy/financing reconstruction is treated as interpretation rather than direct wording.
- **RES-0001 source returns:** B, H.
- **Evidence character:** DIRECT EVIDENCE + CROSS-CASE SYNTHESIS
- **Disagreements / variant readings:** The preceding a-si-rum in SAOC 44, 25 may be a personal name; contributor status and actual move-in for the tenancy example remain unresolved.
- **Confidence:** **HIGH for the textual distinction; MODERATE for the broader TA cross-case synthesis**
- **Confidence rationale:** One text has explicit residence wording, while the comparison records document different relations to housing without equivalent residence wording.
- **Limits on generalization:** No occupant count, prevalence estimate, or inference that contributors/tenants did or did not reside there.
- **Relevance to simulation questions (descriptive only):** Keeps residence, ownership, financing, and access analytically separate.
- **Design status:** NO DESIGN DECISION

### HEC-0003 — Legal continuity does not establish continuous co-residence or an archaeological address

- **Claim / proposition:** Successive kinship, inheritance, lease, and estate-division records can establish durable legal relationships without independently establishing an unchanged co-resident group or a securely identified excavated residence.
- **Period / date range:** Lipit-Enlil through later Samsu-iluna examples in the inspected dossiers.
- **Geography / archive / context:** Selected Nippur legal/prosopographic dossiers; NN 50 and OIMA 1 nos. 28–29 as provenance controls.
- **Underlying primary evidence:** C-return directly read dossier records D01–D18 and D25; NN 50; OIMA 1 nos. 28–29.
- **Relevant secondary scholarship:** Meinhold 2014/2015; Van Wyk 2019a for a stronger continuity interpretation; Stone 1987 and Stone 1976 for catalogue/provenance context.
- **RES-0001 source returns:** C, F.
- **Evidence character:** SOURCE-CRITICAL SYNTHESIS
- **Disagreements / variant readings:** Shared residence or physical continuity may have existed; the supplied evidence does not independently demonstrate it.
- **Confidence:** **MODERATE**
- **Confidence rationale:** The legal sequences are real, but the residential inference requires an additional evidentiary bridge not supplied by the cited documents/provenance records.
- **Limits on generalization:** Not a denial of household continuity and not a universal definition of household.
- **Relevance to simulation questions (descriptive only):** Defines the evidentiary boundary between legal continuity and residential continuity.
- **Design status:** NO DESIGN DECISION

### HEC-0004 — A deposited document can outlast the transaction it records

- **Claim / proposition:** Later Nippur deposits can contain older legal records, so the date and named parties of a deed do not automatically date or identify the resident group associated with the later deposit.
- **Period / date range:** Older records retained in TA XI-2; office-transfer sequence in Samsu-iluna 23–24.
- **Geography / archive / context:** TA House N (NN 35–37) and TB House O (NN 83–84).
- **Underlying primary evidence:** Catalogued dates, findspots, and linked parties for NN 35–37 and NN 83–84.
- **Relevant secondary scholarship:** Stone 1987, catalogue pp. 217–221.
- **RES-0001 source returns:** F.
- **Evidence character:** ARCHAEOLOGICAL/DOCUMENTARY SYNTHESIS
- **Disagreements / variant readings:** Retention is plausible but not the only depositional explanation; displacement is possible for some House N material.
- **Confidence:** **MODERATE**
- **Confidence rationale:** Multiple date/context contrasts are documented, but depositional processes are reconstructed through later publication.
- **Limits on generalization:** No universal archive-retention practice and no automatic resident succession.
- **Relevance to simulation questions (descriptive only):** Separates document biography from occupation history.
- **Design status:** NO DESIGN DECISION

### HEC-0005 — Didactic legal texts are not event registers

- **Claim / proposition:** Nippur didactic/model legal texts can attest legal vocabulary and scribal forms without certifying that the depicted transaction or lawsuit occurred as a contemporary event.
- **Period / date range:** Old Babylonian; the cited school witnesses are undated or broadly dated.
- **Geography / archive / context:** Nippur-attributed school/model texts.
- **Underlying primary evidence:** P368911 (model-contract obverse with mathematical reverse, indexed catalogue/transliteration); PBS 8/2, 173 (CBS 07178; P230709), school court-case text.
- **Relevant secondary scholarship:** Old Babylonian Model Contracts project; Spada 2014; Spada and Hallo 2024 as genre controls where cited by the returns.
- **RES-0001 source returns:** A, H.
- **Evidence character:** DIRECT GENRE EVIDENCE + CROSS-CASE SYNTHESIS
- **Disagreements / variant readings:** A didactic text may transmit material derived from real legal practice; genre alone does not prove every narrative detail was invented. P368911 was not directly image-collated in return A.
- **Confidence:** **HIGH for the genre/event distinction**
- **Confidence rationale:** Both evidence streams explicitly identify school/model contexts; that supports use for form/vocabulary but not event counting.
- **Limits on generalization:** Does not imply that all undated, formulaic, or unsealed texts are exercises.
- **Relevance to simulation questions (descriptive only):** Prevents school texts from inflating counts of historical transactions.
- **Design status:** NO DESIGN DECISION

### HEC-0008 — Late Old Babylonian activity cannot be reduced to one documentary cessation

- **Claim / proposition:** Late Old Babylonian evidence discussed in the supplied reanalysis shows that the cessation of one early documentary sequence cannot by itself be treated as proof that every form of Nippur activity ended at the same moment.
- **Period / date range:** Late Old Babylonian, especially the period around the Sealand-era disruption.
- **Geography / archive / context:** Nippur and connected Dūr-Abi-ešuh cultic/administrative evidence; not a continuous residential sequence.
- **Underlying primary evidence:** HS 189 and CUSAS texts as discussed and reanalyzed in Béranger 2023a; no fresh tablet collation in the supplied return.
- **Relevant secondary scholarship:** Béranger 2023a; Charpin 2020 is preserved as an alternative interpretation reported indirectly through Béranger.
- **RES-0001 source returns:** A.
- **Evidence character:** SECONDARY HISTORICAL SYNTHESIS
- **Disagreements / variant readings:** Archive movement, institutional place references, and the exact endpoint of different kinds of activity remain debated.
- **Confidence:** **MODERATE**
- **Confidence rationale:** The proposition is supported by specialist reanalysis but not by independent collation of the underlying late texts in the package.
- **Limits on generalization:** Does not establish uninterrupted household occupation, a single citywide abandonment date, or the continuity of every institution.
- **Relevance to simulation questions (descriptive only):** Keeps chronological endpoints specific to the evidence stream being discussed.
- **Design status:** NO DESIGN DECISION
