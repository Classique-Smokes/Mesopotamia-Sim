# RES-0001R — Dispute and Quarantine Register

**Authority:** Advisory reconciliation only. Nothing in this file resolves a scholarly dispute by agent vote. `NO DESIGN DECISION` applies throughout.

## 1. Quarantined register-ready candidates

### Q-001 — UM 29-16-672 inferred date and archive affiliation

- **Source candidate:** A-RC02.
- **Issue:** Return A reports that the proposed early-Samsu-iluna date and archive connection are inferential, not a preserved date formula or securely excavated archive label.
- **Evidence:** P257098; Peterson, *CDLN* 2019:1, introduction and §2.1.
- **Disposition:** **QUARANTINED.** The historical attribution may be useful as a working hypothesis, but the supplied package does not justify a stable local proposition with an exact date/archive membership.
- **Needed check:** fresh review of the edition, prosopographic arguments, and any independent museum/excavation context.

### Q-002 — Four market-tablet attributions to Dūr-Abi-ešuh

- **Source candidate:** A-RC07.
- **Issue:** Kreitzscheck 2023 and Béranger 2023b disagree over the attribution of four market tablets associated with Dūr-Abi-ešuh. Alternative provenances are themselves inferential.
- **Disposition:** **QUARANTINED.** No disputed market provenance is treated as a securely excavated origin or direct Nippur evidence.
- **Needed check:** object histories, publication arguments, ARCHIBAB/CDLI records, and any physical/provenance evidence independent of dealer-market attribution.

## 2. Retained propositions with material unresolved issues

### D-001 — BE 6/2, 40 date and grammatical agency
Returns B, D, and E discuss the same document. Return D gives Samsu-iluna 13/I/28; B and E preserve the exact day as unresolved. B also flags disputed grammatical agency. `HEC-0009` therefore fixes only year/month confidently and does not infer that repudiation occurred.

### D-002 — PBS 8/2, 116 performance and field-reference interpretation
The sibling-maintenance obligation, title, inheritance consequence, field-alienation restriction, and witness are retained in `HEC-0011`. Actual maintenance performance, shared residence, and the precise interpretation of the field references remain open. Tablet and envelope are one agreement, not two attestations.

### D-003 — SAOC 44, 1 widow-remarriage interpretation
The document’s marriage plus affiliation of three children is retained in `HEC-0012`. The widow-remarriage characterization depends on Stol’s discussion and a Stone-and-Owen concordance that return B did not directly read; it is not promoted as direct text.

### D-004 — Narāmtum / TMH 10, 18 dossier reconstruction
`HEC-0014` and `HEC-0015` preserve explicit adoption, gift, and later-division facts. Competing all-property versus older one-third readings, stronger co-ownership interpretation, intermediate archive coverage, and some dependent-person labels remain disputed or damaged.

### D-005 — MC 3, 51 chronology
`HEC-0029` accepts the published re-edition’s Samsu-iluna 10 date as the best supplied reading and explicitly rejects use of the older year-23 date for this document. Consequences for the wider economic reconstruction remain MODERATE rather than mechanically inherited from the date correction.

### D-006 — OECT 8 seals, names, and cross-document identities
Return C notes revised filiations and name/identity problems in the Mannum-mešu-liṣṣur dossier. `HEC-0026`–`HEC-0029` rely on document-specific clauses first; cross-document prosopographic links are kept at lower confidence where needed.

### D-007 — SAOC 44, 53 release/manumission wording
The release expression is restored and queried and another clause is damaged. `HEC-0020` is therefore MODERATE and limited to the edited temporal distinction among release, continuing service, and later status claims. It does not infer residential departure.

### D-008 — BE 6/2, 8 Nippur attribution
The manumission/payment content is explicit, but the historical find/provenance attribution conflicts. `HEC-0021` remains MODERATE and is labeled only as qualified Nippur-attributed evidence.

### D-009 — BE 6/2, 57 later re-edition
The early edition supports the protected-rights clauses, but the later Stone-and-Owen re-edition was not directly read in return G. Relationships to some named persons and cultic qualification/duty remain open; `HEC-0018` is MODERATE.

### D-010 — SAL-ISḪIB / Ninurta-related title in BE 6/2, 45
The presence of a religious title is retained, but exact translation/interpretation is not fixed. `HEC-0037` does not infer that the field was temple-owned or that the act was a female prebend sale.

### D-011 — BE 6/2, 10 property terminology and institutional route
The narrative of referral and settlement is retained in `HEC-0039`, but detailed property terminology depends on Poebel’s old edition. The royal-referral/council sequence is not generalized into a universal appellate hierarchy.

### D-012 — House I legal-to-architectural correlation
NN 42–47 support a changing property-rights sequence and a final XA-1 assemblage, but NN 44 area, NN 47 catalogue/narrative tension, intra-building dispersal, and the mapping from legal share to physical occupancy remain unresolved. See `HEC-0030` and `HEC-0031`.

### D-013 — House F cross-phase identity
Robson’s and Stone’s published evidence strongly supports earlier instructional activity, but later legal parties are not securely identified as the same people/household. Fragment reuse and the status of some letters remain open. See `HEC-0032`.

### D-014 — House O Atta cache and building ownership
The Atta dossier is strongly associated with the cache/deposit, but full building excavation, depositor identity, building title, and co-residents are unresolved. See `HEC-0033`.

### D-015 — House C subdivision cause
The physical subdivision is published, but no corresponding deed identifies a family partition or household fission as its cause. Original excavation evidence was mediated in return F. See `HEC-0034`.

### D-016 — Late Nippur continuity and abandonment
Béranger’s reanalysis supports `HEC-0008`, but no fresh collation of HS 189/CUSAS texts was performed in return A, and Charpin’s alternative was consulted only indirectly. Different kinds of activity may have different endpoints.

## 3. Didactic/model-text quarantine rule

P368911 and PBS 8/2, 173 are **not quarantined from all historical use**. They are quarantined specifically from being counted as observed transactions/events. `HEC-0005` retains them for scribal/legal vocabulary and genre evidence.

## 4. Archaeological reconstruction rule

Where a proposition depends on Stone 1987 or Robson 2001 for excavation context, the ancient legal text and the reconstructed building/deposit association are kept as separate evidentiary layers. Return F did not perform a complete independent audit of OIP 78 field documentation.

## 5. Comparative evidence outside Old Babylonian Nippur

Comparative items used only as scholarly pointers (for example Yamada 2011/Tell Taban in the BE 6/2, 57 citation chain) are **not** counted as local attestations and do not raise confidence in a Nippur proposition.
