**Date:** 2026-09-19

**Status:** Advisory research aid; no design decisions. This is not an exhaustive census of tablets, houses or archives.

## 3. Source-genre reliability/use matrix

“Strong” means a good kind of evidence for the stated question when authenticity, reading and context are adequate; it is not a blanket reliability score. The limits are analytical conclusions from the evidence table, not claims that every document in a genre has the same formation history.

| Genre / evidence | Strong evidence for | Checks required | Weak evidence for | Principal limitation | Audited route |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Legal contracts and settlements | A specified act, claimed right, named party, witness or formal relation | Whole text; date formula; seal; restorations; transaction role | A complete life history, universal law in practice, all co-residents | Selective occasions, title retention and publication; explicit rule is not universal observance | TX01–TX05, TX08–TX09; A-C08–A-C09, A-C22 |
| Administrative records | The recorded allocation, receipt, obligation or personnel category within its operation | Administrative unit, period, duplicate accounting and identification practices | A private household roster or a city population | The recording institution and accounting purpose select people; access incomplete here | S10; HC09; A-C16 |
| Letters | A sender’s communication, requests, claims and named contacts in an episode | Sender/addressee; date; rhetoric; writing place; archive/findspot | That every claim was fulfilled; that all named people shared one place | Survival and recipient/copy retention; mentioned place is not automatically provenance | S14–S15; LC02; A-C17 |
| Model contracts / school exercises | Taught formulations and categories; possible legal scenarios | Tablet format, opposite face, exercise context and editorial identification | An observed sale, adoption, loan or marriage | Plausible names and values can be instructional; missing dates alone are not diagnostic | TX06; S16–S17; A-C10 |
| Lexical texts | Scribal classification, word equivalences and possible reading controls | Individual witness, local tradition, date and semantic context | A local inventory, ownership frequency or household membership | Later equivalences may differ; a composite can conceal witness variation | S09 commentary; A-C18 |
| Literary texts | Literary representation, vocabulary and a local textual witness | Composition versus witness; joins; manuscript provenance | Ordinary behavior or demography without external support | Narrative and rhetoric are not documentary reporting | TX07; A-C11, A-C18 |
| Architecture and excavated finds | Spatial arrangement, material associations and phase relationships | Area, level, locus, formation process, reuse and publication correlations | Kinship, ownership or household size from walls alone | Uneven excavation; secondary deposition; functional room labels are interpretations | HC01–HC08; A-C03–A-C05 |
| Seals and impressions | Read names, filiations or offices on an identified sealing | Actual legend, attribution and relation to text; placeholder screening | Automatic biological kinship, event participation or cross-record identity | Damaged/reused seals; catalogue placeholders; attribution disputes | S08, S19; A-C07, A-C12 |
| Digital catalogues and concordances | Retrievable identifiers, joins and the current editorial description | Source edition; field precision; update history; missing values | Independent confirmation merely because several sites repeat a record | Broad bins, migrated fields, dummy objects and incomplete context | TX02, TX06–TX07; A-C11–A-C13 |

## 5. Late chronology and contested-provenance cases

### LC01 — A chronological argument requiring multiple kinds of context

**Key witnesses discussed by Béranger:** TMH NF V, 77 = **HS 189**, a date-list; **CUSAS 8, 23–24**, receipts; and late letters including **CUSAS 29, 205**. HS 189’s proposed endpoint is Ammiṣaduqa 16. The date-list is published in an older volume whose broader classification can obscure its relevance. ARCHIBAB **T25758** was identified but not retrieved [S12, especially pp. 30–33].

Read the historical conclusion and its limits in RESULT §4.5 and A-C14. This case is **not** the object TX05. Original copies, exact regnal readings, and Charpin’s complete opposing interpretation were not independently collated in this research.

### LC02 — An attribution corrected after publication

Kreitzscheck publishes **P532986** (beer account) and **P532987** (letter) as probably connected to Dūr-Abi-ešuh [S14]. Béranger’s NABU note challenges those origins and also **CUSAS 8, 56–57**, proposing alternatives including Dilbat for the account grouping. Her argument questions the evidential weight of prosopographic associations, mentioned locations and acquisition sequence [S15, pp. 171–173].

The appropriate status is **disputed attribution**, not a newly secure Dilbat or Nippur findspot. A letter can refer to people or cattle at a named town without thereby documenting the location where its surviving tablet was deposited. The revised ARCHIBAB routes **T26801/T26806** were not retrieved. No independent photo-based re-edition was attempted [A-C15, A-C17].

## 6. Social visibility matrix

This is an assessment of what the inspected genres make visible, not a ranking of population groups or a quantitative account of their actual prevalence.

| Population / role | What may be visible | What remains unjustified | Evidence / claim route |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Named transaction principals | Roles in a particular act; some explicit relatives, rights or obligations | Legal selection does not enumerate everyone connected to the principal | S18–S20; A-C09, A-C22 |
| Women | TX03 supplies a specific positive attestation; other contexts require their own readings | No sex ratio, universal legal capacity, typical family position or residential pattern | S20; A-C09 |
| Enslaved persons and other dependents | Some are named in transfers or inheritance contexts | Appearance in a property clause is not a preserved self-description or complete biography | S09, S20; A-C19 |
| Children and other relatives | A text may use filiation or sibling language for identification or allocation | Legal kin language does not automatically establish age, biological parentage or co-residence | S08–S09, S20; A-C07, A-C22 |
| Witnesses and scribes | Participation or formal association in the attested document | Repeated professional participation need not establish kinship or household membership | S19; A-C11–A-C12, A-C22 |
| Institutional personnel | Potential visibility through administrative and office-related records | The full administrative population is not reconstructed here; absence in legal texts proves little | S10–S11; HC04, HC09; A-C16 |
| Learners and teachers | Educational activity can be inferred from a relevant assemblage | Exercise names are not a class register; discarded/reused tablets are not a complete teaching roster | S07, S23; A-C05, A-C10 |
| Neighbors and wider contacts | A boundary or correspondence can show a limited connection | Adjacency and communication do not themselves prove shared residence or stable social intimacy | S09, S14–S15; A-C17, A-C22 |
| People never entering the retrieved records | No direct attestation in the present sample | Cannot be counted as absent from the historical city | Actual method in RESULT §2; A-C19 |
