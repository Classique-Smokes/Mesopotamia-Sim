## 2. Corpus and method actually used

### 2.1 Evidence layers and the actual work performed

The central evidence is a selectively read **primary edition**, not a new reading of original tablets. Poebel’s published transliterations, translations, commentary, and catalogue were checked against one another. The principal dossiers here are BE 6/2 8, 23, 24, 28, 29, 40, 43, 48, and 51; BE 57 supplies a limited parallel, and BE 4 was examined but its occupational interpretation was withheld. The ancient documents are cited individually. Poebel’s 1909 generalizations about Nippur, adult adoption, payment practices, or residence are not automatically adopted as facts. [P1909]

Object numbers, regnal dates, and edition concordances were cross-checked where accessible through CDLI and Stone and Zimansky’s catalogue. These are different evidentiary operations: a catalogue entry can identify a tablet without supplying a reliable translation of every clause. The 1976 volume contains short descriptions and photographs; it is not the complete text edition initially implied by the broad label “contracts.” [SZ1976]

Modern scholarship was used to identify disagreements and additional texts, especially for the Ur-Pabilsaĝa dossier, nadītu women, teaching, and legal models. The return distinguishes an ancient text read in an accessible edition, a modern scholar’s interpretation of it, a source known only through a citation, and the present report’s own bounded inference. This distinction applies even when the same tablet has several publication numbers.

### 2.2 Chronology and provenance

Dates are retained as **ruler / regnal year / month / day**, using Roman numerals for months. A missing day remains missing. This avoids introducing an unannounced absolute-chronology conversion. Most securely attributed legal examples cluster within Samsu-iluna 4–22; supplementary catalogue entries reach year 28. BE 8 is Rim-Sin I 51. BE 4, Rim-Sin I 23, is an excluded occupational lead rather than a foundation for the conclusions. [P1909, catalogue pp. 145–152; SZ1976; CDLI8; CDLI4]

“Nippur” here is normally **documentary/catalogue provenance**, not proof of an identified house, room, or occupation surface. For the principal cases, this investigation has not independently recovered a secure connection between every person in a document and a particular residential locus. An archive attribution based on people and transactions is also not the same thing as an excavated household group.

BE 8 is separately labeled **attributed Nippur** throughout. Its older Yokha designation and the modern argument for Nippur are retained rather than silently resolved by choosing a database’s headline. The detailed audit is in section 5.1 and the evidence notes. [P1909, pp. 1, 38–39; AMAS2026, p. 116 nn. 7–8; CDLI8]

### 2.3 Access limits and sample bias

The complete texts of **Stone 1987**, **Stone and Owen 1991**, **Goddeeris 2016**, and **Spada 2018** were not retrieved for direct consultation. Publisher records, concordances, and references in accessible scholarship were checked; those operations are labeled separately in the ledger. The institutional record for **Goddeeris 2023** was accessible, but its listed PDF was restricted. The report consequently does not claim a complete reassessment of modern readings or residential archaeology. [S1987; SO1991; G2016; SP2018; G2023]

This is a purposefully selected set of legible legal relationships, not a representative sample of inhabitants. Contracts preserve the interests a transaction or dispute needed to specify. They are particularly informative about named parties, rights, obligations, property, and possible breaches; they frequently leave the labor process, dwelling arrangements, personal age, and unrecorded relationships unspecified. That source limitation is visible in the individual cases below rather than inferred from a supposed census denominator. [P1909, texts 23, 24, 28, 29, 40, 43, 48, 51]

No percentages of enslaved persons, typical household size, average wage, frequency of manumission, or standard age of work have been calculated. There is no defensible denominator for such calculations in this return.
