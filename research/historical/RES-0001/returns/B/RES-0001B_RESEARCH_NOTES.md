# RES-0001B — Research notes and audit trail

**Date:** 2026-09-19. These are research notes reconstructed from the searches and readings performed in this session, not a verbatim browser log or time-stamped laboratory notebook. They accompany the completed report and preserve consequential decisions about evidence handling. They do not contain project design decisions.

## 1. Search and source-access route

The work began from the supplied source leads and used exact publication titles, tablet designations, named dossiers and bibliographies. It prioritized Nippur editions and excavation-related publications over general accounts of Mesopotamian marriage. Discovery moved between institutional catalogue pages, CDLI, public-domain book text, author-uploaded scholarly texts and cited modern editions.

The principal practical obstacles were inconsistent search retrieval, blocked or failed institutional PDF delivery, corrupted text renderings and inaccessible modern monographs. Exact titles and following catalogue links were more productive than some long multi-keyword searches. Container network attempts did not provide a reliable download route. No failed retrieval is represented as a read source, and the package contains no local copies of external scholarship.

CDLI’s `/artifacts/262151` route yielded the complete displayed edition of PBS 8/2 116; the short `/P262151` route did not consistently work. The stable P-number is therefore preserved alongside the successful access URL. The publication page for MC 3 supplied a crucial concordance even though the modern book text was not retrieved.

## 3. High-value verification outcomes

### 3.1 PBS 8/2 116

The complete displayed tablet, envelope and seal edition was read. The record supplies CBS 07112 / P262151 and the date Rīm-Sîn 50/2. The reported parties and maintenance obligation were checked directly rather than copied uncritically from Stone’s discussion. The tablet and envelope were counted as one legal act.

The support clause and the field-alienation restriction are related but separable evidentiary propositions. A narrow support claim can be retained while the exact referent of the field remains open. The case contains an explicit sealing statement. No explicit oath clause was identified, so Stone’s description as sworn was not repeated as independently verified. The female witness identification was checked in both tablet and envelope wording. [S13; B-C09–C10.]

### 3.2 BE 6/2 40 and 48

The report distinguishes a conditional divorce clause from a recorded divorce. It also distinguishes the repeated half-mina amount in BE 6/2 40 from the different treatment of the woman’s nineteen-shekel contribution. Equal nominal additional sums do not make the whole financial outcome equal.

Poebel’s note about grammatical agency in BE 6/2 40 is preserved as his interpretive intervention. For BE 6/2 48, the primary edition’s mother–sons relationship and support clauses are retained; the sons are not automatically called minors, the man is not automatically called a widower, and an unclear bodily sanction is not confidently modernized. The CDLI MC 3 concordance confirms the designation BE 6/2 48 / P283604 despite confusing OCR around headings. [S01, S03, S11.]

### 3.3 Widowhood and pre-existing children

A late concordance check established SAOC 44 1 = MC 3 14 = P224324. This connected Stone’s excavated marriage-plus-three-children record to Stol’s widow/remarriage note. The evidence was upgraded from a vague lead to a specific, recoverable, MODERATE-confidence case. It was not upgraded to a fresh primary translation of widowhood. The foundation find context was retained, preventing a false inference about the exact dwelling occupied by the family. [S02, S06, S11.]

### 3.4 Support of a boy(?)

SAOC 44 40 is a different document from BE 6/2 40. The catalogue concerns support of a boy by Ipquga(?), while the personal-name index also qualifies the boy identification. Both uncertainties remain visible. No orphanhood, guardianship appointment, custody rule or numeric age was added. [S02; B-C14.]

### 3.5 Housing and non-kin affiliation

For SAOC 44 25, the report avoids selecting between the narrative’s eight and catalogue’s nine contributors. Asirum’s reported residence is retained; the other contributors are not all made residents, and missing kin identifications are not converted into positive non-kin classifications.

For SAOC 44 44/46/47, the catalogue’s legal purchase direction is distinguished from Stone’s allocation of individuals to reconstructed houses. The ownership sequence is useful; an actual co-residence-to-separation sequence is not independently established.

SAOC 44 31 supplies an asset link to Iahalpilum, not a proven migration. SAOC 44 50 supplies a one-year rental, not a recovered occupant list. SAOC 44 53 supplies manumission, not a known departure. [S02; B-C15–C19.]

## 4. Identification and citation safeguards

Names were normalized conservatively for readability, not merged into one prosopography. Bēltani, Naramtum, Enlil-nišu and similar recurrent names can designate different people. Father, mother, brother, son, spouse, heir, witness, buyer and resident are retained as different contextual descriptions.

Every bare publication number was expanded where confusion would matter. SAOC 44 is the 1987 book, OIMA 1 is the 1976 publication series/volume, and MC 3 is the 1991 adoption volume. A book’s title saying Nippur does not guarantee every included or discussed tablet has secure Nippur provenance. Museum numbers and dates were not filled from memory when not verified.

The old-language forms in Poebel are not presented as new philological determinations. Regnal dates have priority; modern BCE conversions are explicitly approximate and follow the cited catalogue. No year was extrapolated from a scholar’s “ten years” statement about the marriage dossier.

## 5. Interpretations retained but not resolved

The brideprice/dowry distinction in BE 6/2 47; the stage of the relationship dissolved in BE 6/2 58; exact father/assent interpretation in SAOC 44 34; the field restriction in PBS 8/2 116; the nadītu status of particular women when inferred rather than stated; close-kin marriage as a wider pattern; the full alternative chronology of the Ur-Pabilsaĝa dossier.

## 6. Comparative and didactic filters

Cotsen 52175 was excluded from the direct Nippur event corpus. It is a model-contract tablet of uncertain excavated origin, with a Larsa-related attribution suggested by its oath. Its adoption narrative does not establish an actual Nippur foundling history. BM 80989 belongs to Sippar, not Nippur. Their value is comparative and methodological. CBS 11324 is a Nippur didactic lead not directly consulted. [S07–S08, S20.]
