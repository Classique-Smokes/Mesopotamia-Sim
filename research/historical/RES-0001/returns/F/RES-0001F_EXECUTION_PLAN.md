# RES-0001F — Execution Plan

**Task:** 6 / RES-0001F — Domestic Archaeology, Residence, and Neighborhood  
**Program:** RES-0001 — Old Babylonian Nippur Household Foundations  
**Prepared:** 2026-09-19  
**Status:** Archive review and execution planning complete; historical research not yet undertaken.  
**Authority:** Advisory research plan. Not a canonical project decision or historical result.

## 1. Assignment and governing material

Task 6 corresponds to the sixth workstream in the program’s A–H manifest: RES-0001F. All seven files in its agent package have been read, including the controlling task brief, shared context, evidence/output protocol, source starting points, return template, and canonical Seed copy. The program README, manifest, and dispatch guide have also been reviewed.

The research question is:

> What can the excavated residential evidence of Old Babylonian Nippur—especially where texts and architecture can be linked—tell us about residence, household spatial organization, property change, neighborhood relationships, and the physical continuity or transformation of households?

The approved frame is approximately 1900–1600 BCE, but every archaeological context and document must retain its most precise defensible dating. This date range is a research boundary, not an assumption that all contexts are contemporary or equally documented.

The main task is to test links among buildings, archaeological phases, documents, named people, and social interpretations. Building, property, residence, and household are separate propositions to investigate, not interchangeable labels.

## 2. Scope and independence

Concentrate on TA, TB, the Scribal Quarter, and other relevant Old Babylonian residential contexts identified through source review. Verify the relationship among excavation-area names and publication labels before treating them as separate samples.

The investigation covers plans, access, rooms and courtyards, installations, find distributions, rebuilding, subdivision, tablet findspots, archive associations, property transactions, possible residence patterns, neighborhood relationships, mobility, and continuity. Funerary or cultic material enters only when securely contextualized and directly relevant to the residential question.

Kinship, inheritance, property, labor, institutions, and terminology will be examined only where necessary to establish or test a spatial/documentary association. Broader conclusions belong to the other workstreams or later reconciliation. This research will not depend on receiving the other agents’ conclusions and will not merge or reconcile their reports.

No household population estimates from floor area alone; no kinship from proximity alone; no automatic equation of a house with a household. No simulation mechanics, entity designs, canonical membership rules, or canonical HE-number assignments.

## 3. Research sequence

### Stage 1 — Establish the source corpus and access record

Start with the packet’s discovery leads, verifying their bibliographic details and their actual coverage during execution:

- McCown and Haines, *Nippur I* (1967), for excavation documentation, plans, stratigraphy, and recorded contexts.
- Stone, *Nippur Neighborhoods* (1987), for neighborhood analysis and the underlying architectural, documentary, and person-identification arguments.
- Stone, “Texts, Architecture and Ethnographic Analogy: Patterns of Residence in Old Babylonian Nippur” (1981), for residence interpretations and the role of analogy.
- Stone, *Old Babylonian Contracts from Nippur* (1976), and relevant underlying editions, for specific documents used in spatial and property reconstructions.

Use CDLI, ORACC/ePSD, museum catalogues, and concordances to identify and cross-check objects and editions. Expand through bibliographies, later specialist scholarship, corrections, and alternative interpretations; do not treat the supplied leads as exhaustive or independently verified research findings.

Keep a source ledger from the beginning. Record full citation, stable identifiers, access location, exact pages/figures/plates used, scope, and whether consultation was direct. Distinguish primary documentation embedded in a synthetic work from that work’s interpretation. Several publications repeating one excavation observation or tablet do not become several independent attestations.

Relevant but inaccessible works will be marked as identified but not consulted. Search snippets and second-hand citations will not stand in for examination of the underlying evidence.

**Output:** Source ledger and a coverage/access map showing which contexts can actually be investigated.

### Stage 2 — Build the archaeological chronology and context framework

Create one research record per relevant house/context and distinguish its successive phases. Record excavation area, house designation, level, locus, dating basis, preservation, and publication reference. Reconcile naming differences across publications without erasing disagreements.

Maintain separate fields for:

1. the original date of a document, including its regnal attribution where available;
2. its findspot and depositional context;
3. the architectural phase and dating of that context;
4. the interval of occupation or ownership inferred by an excavator or later scholar.

These dates may converge, but that convergence must be demonstrated. A tablet’s date alone will not date all associated walls or establish the identity of the residents.

Inspect published plans and plates alongside the text. Record entrances, circulation, blocked doorways, wall additions, subdivisions, rebuilding, installations, and finds only as securely as the publication permits. Attribute room-function labels separately to original excavators, later reinterpretations, or direct material support.

Where find distributions are incompletely published or recovery is uneven, record that limitation rather than treating blank spaces as evidence of absent activity.

**Output:** House/context–phase matrix, architectural observations, and a chronology/nomenclature crosswalk.

### Stage 3 — Audit document-to-building and person-to-residence associations

For each consequential association, test three different questions:

- Is the tablet’s location and phase securely documented?
- Does the document refer to the excavated property, another identifiable property, or an uncertain location?
- Does the named person appear as an owner, buyer, seller, heir, tenant, witness, other participant, or demonstrable resident?

Record the publication or museum identifier, corpus identifier where available, genre, language, edition/translation, date, people, property description, boundaries, and the evidence used to identify the property or person. Do not merge similarly named people without a supporting identification argument.

Separate actual transaction documents from school exercises and model contracts. A didactic text may illuminate categories and formulae, but is not evidence that the transaction occurred.

Consider alternative explanations for a text’s presence: retention of older documents, transferred archives, displaced objects, secondary deposits, or uncertain excavation association. Record an apparent match between a legal change and a building alteration as correlation unless the temporal and contextual evidence supports a stronger connection.

**Output:** An explicit audit trail from document to context to person/property interpretation, including failed and uncertain links.

### Stage 4 — Develop a stratified case study and comparisons

Select the strongest available case only after the source audit. Prioritize a context with documented architectural succession, recoverable text/find associations, defensible dates, and identifiable persons or transactions. Do not preselect a socially appealing example or promise that all desired links will be recoverable.

The required case-study table will use the packet’s structure:

| House/context | Phase/date | Texts/finds | Documented people/transactions | Architectural change | Interpretation | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| To be populated from reviewed evidence | | | | | | |

Accompany the table with precise citations and a narrative separating observation, published interpretation, and this report’s inference. Where possible, add a contrasting context that tests whether the principal case’s pattern depends on distinctive preservation, dating, or archive survival.

Test questions rather than assume answers: Does a partition correspond to architectural subdivision? Does continued architecture accompany changes in documented property holders? Does rebuilding coincide with continued documentary associations? Are adjoining people demonstrably related, or only spatially close?

At least one stratified case linking architecture to named people/documents will be included if evidence permits. When no sufficiently secure case is recoverable, present the strongest partial case, identify the missing link, and explain the evidentiary limit without manufacturing continuity.

**Output:** Case-study table and bounded interpretation, with a contrasting case where supportable.

### Stage 5 — Analyze residence, continuity, and neighborhood claims

Distinguish continuity of walls or plots, continuity of property rights, continuity of named people, and continuity of a household or kin group. Evidence for one will not be silently promoted into evidence for another.

Evaluate residence, possible kin clustering, neighborhood persistence, mobility, and changes in domestic activity against chronological differences and competing explanations. Spatial proximity is not itself kinship; documentary participation is not itself residence; ownership is not itself household membership.

For every important inference, state the evidence supporting it, the alternative explanation, and the observation needed to discriminate between them. Preserve disagreements between excavation reports, textual reconstructions, and later interpretations. Identify where an argument relies on ethnographic analogy rather than Nippur evidence.

Evidence from other cities or periods will be placed in a separate comparative section with its own place/date, explanatory purpose, and limit. Analogies may generate questions but will not fill gaps in the direct Nippur record.

**Output:** Claim-level analysis of patterns, variation, exceptions, and limits—not a single generalized model of Nippur households.

### Stage 6 — Assemble and audit the research return

Use local claim IDs such as `F-C01`. Retain the shared evidence-table fields: claim ID, claim, period/date, Nippur context, evidence type, primary evidence, secondary interpretation, counterevidence/disagreement, confidence, generalization limit, project relevance, and design status.

Apply confidence to each narrowly stated proposition within its context:

- **HIGH:** converging primary examples and/or strong contextual evidence with limited serious dispute.
- **MODERATE:** supported but materially constrained by sample, interpretation, chronology, or source bias.
- **LOW:** suggestive, sparse, disputed, weakly contextualized, or dependent on substantial reconstruction.

Separate strong observations from weaker social interpretations rather than giving a compound claim the confidence of its strongest component. Confidence never removes a geographical, temporal, or social boundary.

Audit every substantive claim for recoverable citation, dates, provenance, source genre, direct-versus-inferred status, contradictory evidence, and explicit limits. Recheck plan/plate references and tablet concordances. Ensure repeated citations do not inflate the number of independent examples.

**Output:** A checked, independent research return for the Master Architect.

## 4. Planned deliverables

The principal deliverable is `RES-0001F_RESULT.md`, using all nine sections of the supplied return template:

1. Executive findings: 5–12 concise findings with confidence, scope, and best supporting sources.
2. Corpus and method actually used: actual dates, contexts, sources, sample biases, and access limitations.
3. Full claim-by-claim evidence table.
4. Analysis, including the required stratified case study and context–phase table.
5. Disagreement, ambiguity, and source limitations.
6. Clearly separated comparative evidence.
7. Unresolved questions and recommended research follow-up.
8. Register-ready candidate claims, without canonical HE numbers.
9. Full source ledger, including direct-consultation status.

A supplementary `RES-0001F_APPENDICES.md` may hold expanded phase tables, findspot/document concordances, and source notes. The principal report will remain self-contained and will retain its required evidence table and ledger.

All historical candidate claims remain advisory and carry `NO DESIGN DECISION`. The independent report is returned without merging the other seven workstreams or editing canonical project registers.

## 5. Completion criteria

The research return is ready when every specified scope item is addressed with evidence or an explicit account of what could not be established; at least one qualifying stratified case is supplied where possible, or the conditional limitation is documented; and all consequential claims can be traced to identifiable evidence.

The final audit must also confirm that archaeological phases have not been conflated, documentary dates have not become occupation dates without argument, household composition has not been inferred from floor area or proximity, comparisons remain separate, and meaningful uncertainty survives the synthesis.

Completeness means an auditable answer to the bounded question, not forced certainty or an invented citywide pattern.
